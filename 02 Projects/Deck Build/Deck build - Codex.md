# Freestanding residential deck plan — Blue Springs, Missouri

  

**Deck:** exactly 31'-0" × 21'-0" outside-to-outside; finished surface exactly 24" above finished grade

**Code/pricing research checked:** July 15, 2026

**Plan status:** construction-ready layout for municipal review, but **not an issued permit or engineered seal**

  

> **Critical design change.** A conventional dropped 3-ply 2×8 beam cannot be responsibly fitted below 2×8 joists at this deck’s fixed 24-inch elevation. With 5/4 decking it leaves only 5½ inches between a concrete pier 2 inches above grade, a 1-inch post-base standoff, and the bottom of the beam; 2×6 decking leaves only 5 inches. The specified Simpson ABU66Z base is itself about 6-1/16 inches high, before the post cap is added. This plan therefore uses the strongest practical low-profile alternative: **three flush triple-2×8 beams with joists in hangers**. Do not revert to dropped beams without a building-department-approved connection detail or an engineer-designed steel saddle.

  

## 1. Code, permit, and local requirements

  

### Confirmed current Blue Springs requirements

  

- The City states that permit applications submitted on or after June 1, 2026 must comply with the **2024 International Residential Code (IRC)**. See the [Blue Springs Codes Administration page](https://www.bluespringsgov.com/222/Codes-Administration-Division).

- Ordinance 5480, adopted February 18, 2026, retains these local climatic values: **20 psf ground snow**, **115 mph wind**, Seismic Design Category A, severe weathering, **36-inch frost-line depth**, moderate-to-heavy termite hazard, and 6°F winter design temperature. See [Ordinance 5480](https://ecode360.com/BL3243/laws/LF2617498.pdf), especially ordinance pages 16–17.

- The ordinance retains the permit exemption for uncovered platforms/decks/walks **not more than 30 inches above grade and not over a basement or story**. This 24-inch deck appears to satisfy that text. Ordinance page 22 and [Chapter 500, §500.020](https://ecode360.com/28249653) are the controlling municipal-code sources.

- The City’s current Codes Administration webpage separately says construction on “a deck” requires a building permit. That conflicts with the ordinance’s under-30-inch exemption and an older 2023 pamphlet. **Resolve the conflict in writing with Codes Administration, 816-228-0118, before work.** Do not rely only on the exemption.

- Zoning/setbacks still apply even if a building permit is waived. The City directs applicants to Planning at 816-228-0117 because setbacks vary by building type. An uncovered deck may encroach up to 30% into a required setback under the published pamphlet, but the parcel-specific setback and easements must be confirmed. See the [Home Improvement Permit pamphlet](https://www.bluespringsgov.com/DocumentCenter/View/17040/Home-Improvement-Permit-Pamplet-Updated-2023).

- If a permit is required, the City accepts applications through its [Permit & Application Center](https://www.bluespringsgov.com/1603/Permit-Application-Center). The old deck pamphlet lists footing and final inspections; the current webpage says all permitted work receives applicable inspections and final approval. Ask the plan reviewer whether this flush-beam layout also needs framing inspection before decking. Inspection hotline: 816-228-0104.

- Inspection requests must be made by 3 p.m. the preceding day under the June 2026 update. Do not cover footings until the footing inspection is released.

  

### Standard 2024 IRC criteria used

  

- Residential deck live load: **40 psf**; conventional wood-deck tables include **10 psf dead load** and wet-service factors.

- No guard is required where the walking surface is not more than 30 inches above grade measured within 36 inches horizontally of the edge. This plan has no guard because the stated grade is flat and the surface is 24 inches high. Recheck after final grading.

- 5/4 (actual approximately 1-inch) wood decking installed perpendicular to joists is permitted at up to 16 inches o.c.; nominal 2-inch wood decking is permitted at up to 24 inches o.c.

- Southern Pine No. 2 2×8 deck joists at 16 inches o.c. have an IRC table span of **11'-10"** at 40 psf live load. This plan’s main clear span is only **9'-1½"**.

- Beam and joist selections are also checked against the [AWC DCA6 deck guide](https://awc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/AWC-DCA62015-DeckGuide-1804.pdf), which uses 40 psf live, 10 psf dead, No. 2 lumber, wet service, and L/360. DCA6 is supporting guidance, not a substitute for the adopted 2024 IRC.

- Beam-to-post gravity load must bear on the post through an approved post cap; a beam may not simply be bolted to the side of the post. See the [American Wood Council explanation](https://awc.org/faq/how-are-deck-beams-or-girders-attached-to-posts/).

  

### Conservative assumptions requiring City confirmation

  

- Soil bearing is assumed to be **1,500 psf** on undisturbed native soil. Confirm actual allowable bearing and the final 22-inch footing diameter with Blue Springs. Expansive fill, soft soil, groundwater, previous excavation, or uncontrolled fill invalidates the footing design.

- Footing bottoms are set at **38 inches below finished grade**, 2 inches below the confirmed 36-inch frost line. Confirm whether the inspector measures to bottom of pad and whether a belled pad/12-inch shaft detail is accepted.

- The freestanding lateral/uplift scheme—rated bases and caps, fully fastened joist hangers, corner knee braces, and underside diagonal straps—must be approved by the plan reviewer. The IRC does not supply one universal prescriptive lateral detail for every fully freestanding configuration.

- All structural lumber is No. 2 or better Southern Yellow Pine, pressure treated for exterior wet service. Use **UC4A/ground-contact treatment for posts and any member within 6 inches of soil**. Above-ground UC3B decking is acceptable where the product label permits deck use; ground-contact decking is also acceptable.

### Why 16-inch spacing is the best value

  

The 31-foot framing width is divided into 24 equal bays, so actual spacing is 370.5 ÷ 24 = **15.4375 inches**. A 12-inch layout needs 32 base joist lines plus extra framing at both end borders and at the breaker, roughly 36 lines rather than 28. It adds about 16 joist pieces, 48 hangers, fasteners, and labor. Because 2×8 joists span only 9'-1½" (77% of their 11'-10" table limit), the chosen 16-inch layout already provides a modest stiffness upgrade. Twelve-inch spacing makes the surface slightly firmer but is poor value here.

  

### Load and member checks

  

**Design loads.** Option A uses 40 psf live + 10 psf dead = **50 psf**. Option B is checked at 40 + 12 = **52 psf** to recognize the heavier 2×6 decking. The 20 psf local ground snow load is lower than the 40 psf live load and is not combined with live load.

  

**Joist check, worst case (Option B).** Tributary width at 16 inches = 1.333 ft. Uniform joist load = 52 × 1.333 = **69.3 plf**. At 9.125 ft clear span, simple-span moment = wL²/8 = **721 lb-ft**. Actual span is 2'-8½" shorter than the 11'-10" IRC table limit. Outriggers are only 7½ inches and are supported by hangers, so no joist cantilever calculation is needed.

  

**Interior beam.** Its tributary width is 9.5 ft, so Option B line load = 52 × 9.5 = **494 plf**. For a 6-ft post spacing, M = wL²/8 = **2,223 lb-ft** and end reaction = wL/2 = **1,482 lb**. A triple 2×8 has section modulus 3 × (1.5 × 7.25² ÷ 6) = **39.42 in³**, requiring only 677 psi adjusted bending capacity. Shear demand is approximately 68 psi. Deflection using E = 1.26 million psi is approximately 0.08 inch, about L/900. These values are below normal wet-service No. 2 Southern Pine capacities; the City must nevertheless approve the built-up flush-beam calculation because the interior beam supports joists on both sides.

  

**Outer beams.** Tributary width = 1.0-ft perimeter strip + 4.75 ft = **5.75 ft**; Option B line load = 299 plf, substantially below the interior beam.

  

**Posts and footings.** Conservative interior-post tributary load = 494 plf × 6 ft = **2,964 lb**, plus a small beam/post self-weight allowance. A 22-inch round pad has area π × 11² ÷ 144 = **2.64 ft²** and nominal 1,500-psf bearing capacity = **3,960 lb**. The capacity-to-calculated-load ratio is about 1.30. Final diameter depends on actual soil and City approval.

## 6. Decking layouts — both options

  

Both options use actual 5½-inch board width. Verify every bundle before framing; if the measured average differs, redo the course calculation.

  

### Common exact layout

  

- End picture-frame boards: one 5½-inch course at X = 0–5.5" and one at X = 366.5–372".

- Center breaker: one 5½-inch board centered at X = 186", occupying X = 183.25–188.75".

- Provide four 1/8-inch longitudinal separation gaps: end border/field and field/breaker on each side.

- Each field-board cut length = (372 − 3×5.5 − 4×0.125) ÷ 2 = **177.5" = 14'-9½"**. Every field piece comes from 16-foot stock; no random field butt joints.

- Across the 21-foot width use **43 full field courses** between two 5½-inch long picture-frame borders. Board width total = 45 × 5.5 = 247.5 inches. Remaining gap total = 252 − 247.5 = 4.5 inches over 44 spaces, average **0.1023 inch**.

- Practical balanced story pole: use 32 gaps at 3/32 inch and 12 gaps at 1/8 inch, distributed symmetrically (for example, insert a 1/8 gap about every fourth space and mirror the sequence from center). Total = 32×3/32 + 12×1/8 = **4.5 inches exactly**. Measure actual boards first; wet boards may need smaller installation gaps to reach the same final total.

- Set the outer fascia/decking control lines first. Install field courses from both long sides toward the center and use a story pole; do not rip a narrow final board. The calculation yields 43 full-width courses.

- Long picture-frame sides fit between the two full-width end borders: net 361 inches. Use two equal 180-7/16-inch pieces with a 1/8-inch center joint on solid blocking.

- Each 21-foot end border: 12-foot piece + 107-7/8-inch piece + 1/8-inch joint = 252 inches.

- Center breaker between long borders: 240¾ inches; use 12-foot piece + 96-5/8-inch piece + 1/8-inch joint.

  

### Option A — 5/4×6 decking

  

- Install crown/bark side consistently; two #9 × 2½-inch ACQ-rated exterior deck screws at every supporting joist/block, approximately ¾ inch from each board edge.

- Predrill all fasteners within 1 inch of an end, every screw in picture-frame/breaker pieces, and any hole near a knot. Use a bit approximately 1/64 inch smaller than the screw root.

- Minimum screws calculated approximately 2,260; buy **2,500** (10%+ allowance).

- Best value: lighter, easier to handle, permitted at the selected spacing, and currently the lowest verified decking cost.

  

### Option B — 2×6 decking

  

- Same board count and layout; use #9 or #10 × **3-inch** ACQ-rated deck screws, two at every support.

- Predrilling requirements are the same; predrill every board end because the thicker material is more likely to split near butt joints.

- Buy **2,500** screws.

- Structural change: post cuts are 12¼ inches instead of 12¾ inches. Footings, beams, joists, blocking, posts, and connectors remain unchanged because they were checked at 52 psf.

  

For both options use 305 stainless screws where the deck will receive deicing salt or frequent chemical exposure; otherwise use a manufacturer-labeled polymer/triple-coated or hot-dip-galvanized fastener approved for the exact treatment chemical. Never mix stainless fasteners with incompatible plated connectors in direct wet contact.

  

## 7. Fascia and cut-end treatment

  

- Fascia: ¾-inch actual × 7¼-inch PT 1×8, covering all four rim faces and the exposed ends of the three beams. Use eight 16-foot boards including overage.

- Keep fascia top flush with framing top and decking/fascia outside faces on the 31×21 control lines. Use 45-degree outside corners or square-butt corners with the meeting piece shortened by twice the ¾-inch thickness; do not add dimensions twice.

- Leave 1/8 inch at intermediate fascia butt joints and approximately 1/16 inch at dry-weather miter joints. Provide solid blocking behind every joint.

- Fasten with two vertical rows of #9 × 2½-inch ACQ-rated fascia/trim screws at 24 inches o.c.; 120 screws including allowance. Predrill fascia ends.

- Brush two flood coats of **copper naphthenate end-cut preservative** meeting AWPA M4 on every field cut, notch, and drilled end grain before assembly. One gallon is ample. Do not use untreated ripped stock as exterior blocking.

  

## 8. Ground preparation

  

- Exact covered area: 31 × 21 = **651 ft²**.

- Fabric purchase: 651 × 1.10 = **716.1 ft²**; buy at least 800 ft² to permit 12-inch overlaps and pier cuts.

- Compacted gravel at 3 inches: 651 × 0.25 ÷ 27 = **6.03 yd³ compacted**.

- Add 10% waste plus about 10% loose-to-compacted allowance: 6.03 × 1.20 = **7.24 yd³**; order **7.4 yd³**, approximately **10.4 tons** at 1.4 tons/yd³. Confirm the quarry’s actual density.

- Use clean, angular **¾-inch crushed limestone or crushed rock with limited fines**, not pea gravel. It locks together and drains while discouraging vegetation.

  

Sequence: remove sod/organic material; excavate approximately 3 inches while maintaining positive drainage; pour/inspect footings; fine-grade soil to drain at least 1/8 inch per foot toward a safe outlet and never toward the house; compact subgrade; install nonwoven landscape fabric with 12-inch overlaps and seal/cut neatly around piers; place gravel in two lifts; compact each lift; restore the top to the original finished-grade datum. Keep gravel below the concrete tops and out of post bases. Low-profile metal/plastic edging at the perimeter is optional where gravel migration is likely; do not create a dam that traps water.

## 12. Construction sequence

  

1. **Verify authority.** Obtain written permit determination, parcel setbacks/easements, HOA approval, soil/footing acceptance, and lateral-detail acceptance. Contact [Missouri 811](https://missouri-811.org/place-a-locate-request/) at least three working days before excavation; private lines may require a separate locator. Never excavate until every utility has responded.

2. **Establish footprint.** Set offset batter boards beyond the 31×21 rectangle. Protect a benchmark representing finished grade 0.00 and a laser benchmark at deck surface +24.00.

3. **Square it.** Set X strings 31'-0" and Y strings 21'-0". Adjust until both diagonals are 37'-5 5/16" and equal within 1/8". Recheck after every string movement.

4. **Mark footings.** Plumb down all 18 coordinates from crossing strings. Mark 22-inch pads and larger working excavations. A common error is measuring from framing rather than the finished (0,0) corner.

5. **Excavate.** Dig to undisturbed bearing at least 38 inches below finished grade. Hand-dig in utility tolerance zones. Protect open holes from falls, rain, and collapse.

6. **Form/pour.** Form an 8-inch-thick, 22-inch round pad from -38 to -30 and a centered 12-inch shaft from -30 to +2. Use 4,000-psi bag mix at the labeled water ratio; do not add water for workability. Place monolithically where practical, consolidate, and keep soil out. **Inspection:** footing excavation/reinforcement (if required) before concrete.

7. **Set anchors/bases.** Template-set centered 5/8-inch HDG anchors while concrete is plastic, or use an approved post-installed system only after cure. After specified cure, install ABU66Z bases level and aligned; do not use bases to compensate for badly misplaced piers.

8. **Posts.** Laser the three beam-bottom elevations. Cut/treat posts to 12¾ inches (A) or 12¼ inches (B), but field-measure every pier and trim individually so beam bottoms land exactly at +15¾/+15¼. Do not assume all pier tops are identical.

9. **Build beams.** Crown plies upward, stagger splices over specified posts, clamp straight, and install two staggered rows of SDW screws at 24 inches o.c. from each outer face. Keep ends at X=.75" and 371.25". Set in BCS caps and install only approved connector fasteners.

10. **Joists/rims/blocking/bracing.** Lay 24 equal 15-7/16-inch bays. Hang 56 main joists; install 56 offcut outriggers at outside faces of B1/B3; install outer rims; double end-border and center-breaker lines; install 96 bay blocks and bridge blocks. Treat cuts before fastening.

11. **Check geometry/elevation.** Verify 370.5×250.5 framing, square diagonals, beam/post plumb, and framing-top elevations +23 (A) or +22½ (B). Plane only minor high crowns; replace badly crowned/twisted pieces.

12. **Ground work.** After footing release and before decking blocks access, excavate/grade the underdeck area. Keep soil away from bases.

13. **Fabric/gravel.** Install 800 ft² fabric with 12-inch laps, then compact 7.4 yd³ loose rock in two lifts to final grade 0.00. Maintain drainage.

14. **Picture-frame/breaker support.** Verify solid support beneath every board edge, end, butt joint, and the four 1/8-inch longitudinal gaps. The center and end strips require doubled framing; long borders require a continuous inner blocking row.

15. **Dry layout.** Measure 10–20 boards face-to-face to determine average actual width. Mark the 43-course story pole and four longitudinal field limits. Recalculate if average width is not 5½ inches.

16. **Field decking.** Install 86 pieces at 14'-9½", working symmetrically. Use two screws at each support. Snap lines so screw rows remain straight; do not force bowed boards by widening the deck.

17. **Breaker.** Install the 12-foot and 8'-0⅝" pieces with a 1/8-inch supported joint, centered at X=15'-6". Predrill.

18. **Picture frame.** Install end borders and long borders to the fixed 31×21 outside control lines. Measure outside-to-outside before final screws. A common error is adding fascia thickness after already setting a 31×21 frame.

19. **Fascia.** Install ¾×7¼ fascia flush with framing top; outside faces define the exact finished rectangle. Two rows of fascia screws at 24 inches o.c.; back all joints.

20. **Final checks.** Verify every connector hole required by its schedule is filled, anchors/bolts are tight, no untreated cut is exposed, drainage is open, surface is +24, dimensions are 31×21, and no edge exceeds the guard threshold after grading. Prepare footing photos, product labels, connector schedules, and this coordinate plan for final inspection.

  

Safety: use trench/open-hole barriers, eye/hearing/respiratory protection, gloves for treated lumber and concrete, GFCI-protected tools, proper lifting for wet lumber, and temporary bracing until the deck is self-supporting. Never work under an unsupported beam.

## 14. Final comparison and recommendation

  

| Choice | Cost/weight | Stiffness/installation | Recommendation |

|---|---|---|---|

| 5/4×6 | Lowest verified cost; lighter | Code-permitted at 16"; easy handling; 2½" screws | **Best value** |

| 2×6 | About $480 more in the HD basket; roughly 50% thicker/heavier | Firmer board feel, 3" screws, harder to straighten; framing unchanged | Choose only if thicker appearance/impact resistance is worth cost |

| 16" o.c. | 28 framed lines | Main joists at only 77% of table span; good stiffness | **Best value** |

| 12" o.c. | Roughly 36 lines; more lumber and ~48 additional hangers | Firmer surface, especially as decking weathers | Optional upgrade, not economical here |

  

**Recommended final configuration:** 5/4×6 PT SYP decking; 2×8 PT SYP No. 2 joists at actual 15-7/16 inches o.c.; three flush triple-2×8 beams; 18 short 6×6 UC4A posts; 22-inch pads with 12-inch shafts to 38 inches below grade; full picture frame; one center breaker; face-driven ACQ-rated screws. This balances common stock, modest stiffness reserve, uncomplicated geometry, cost, and long-term serviceability.

  

## 15. Consistency audit and stop-work conditions

  

- Geometry: 31×21 finished dimensions, 30'-10½" framing, ¾" fascia, 43 field courses, 86 field pieces, 18 footings, 3 beams, 6 posts/beam, and all coordinates agree.

- Elevation: +24 surface; +23/+22½ framing top; +15¾/+15¼ framing bottom; +3 post bottom; 12¾/12¼ posts. Flush beams fit; dropped beams do not.

- Loads: Option B’s 52 psf governs joists, beams, posts, and footings. The 20 psf local snow load is lower than live load.

- Concrete: 22" × 8" pad plus 12" × 32" shaft is about 3.85 ft³ per footing; 18 footings need about 116 80-lb bags, and 128 includes about 10%.

- Gravel: 651 ft² × 3 inches = 6.03 compacted yd³; 7.4 yd³ purchase includes waste/compaction.

- The largest material-price uncertainty is retailer-local stock and pricing; Lowe’s was confirmed incomplete, and Home Depot/Menards quantities require store verification.

  

**Mandatory cautions:** underground utilities must be located before excavation. Final footing diameter/depth depends on verified soil and Blue Springs approval. The Blue Springs building department must approve the final design or confirm exemption in writing. A Missouri-licensed structural engineer must review any condition outside prescriptive deck provisions, including poor soil, unusual wind exposure, altered elevations, roofs, hot tubs, heavy equipment, or a rejected lateral detail. Verify lumber species, grade stamp, treatment category, retention, straightness, moisture, and actual dimensions before purchase. Retail prices and inventory change.
