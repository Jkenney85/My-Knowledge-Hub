## 2.2 Standard IRC requirements applied in this design (not Blue-Springs-specific)

These come from the IRC (2021/2024 deck provisions, Section R507, whose 40 psf live-load
span values are unchanged in substance from the 2018 tables the City previously
enforced). Where the 2024 IRC is cited, verify the exact table values with the City
plan reviewer, since Anthropic's assistant compiled them from the 2018/2021 published
tables and the 2024 edition's deck tables.

* Deck live load 40 psf; deck dead load 10 psf assumed (IRC Table R301.5). Ground snow
  load 20 psf is less than 40 psf live, so the live load governs the design.
* Deck joist spans: IRC Table R507.6 (2x8 SYP No. 2 at 16 in o.c.: 11 ft-10 in max span;
  cantilever up to 1/4 of the actual backspan).
* Deck beam spans: IRC Table R507.5 (3-ply 2x8 SYP No. 2 supporting an 8.5-ft effective
  joist span: approx. 8 ft-11 in allowable span by interpolation between the 8-ft and
  10-ft columns; we use 7 ft-0 in).
* Footings: IRC R507.3; minimum bearing 12 in below undisturbed grade and below frost
  depth (locally 36 in); sized for soil bearing capacity.
* Presumptive soil bearing: IRC Table R401.4.1 gives 1,500 psf for clay/silt soils --
  the conservative default used here (see Section 2.3).
* Guards not required: walking surface less than 30 in above grade (IRC R312.1.1).
* Post base/uplift: posts restrained laterally and against uplift at base and cap
  (IRC R507.4); freestanding decks are exempt from house-attachment lateral-anchor
  requirements but must be stable as a self-supporting structure.
* Wood protection: AWPA UC4A ("Ground Contact") treatment where wood is within 6 in of
  ground, is hard to replace, or has poor ventilation/drainage (IRC R317; AWPA U1).
  Because this entire frame sits 5 in to 16 in above the gravel, ground-contact
  treatment is specified for ALL framing, posts, and beams. Field-cut ends must be
  treated with copper naphthenate (AWPA M4).
* Fasteners and connectors in contact with treated wood: hot-dip galvanized (ASTM A153
  / G185) or stainless steel (IRC R317.3). Do not mix aluminum with treated lumber.

# 4. Structural Design and Calculations

Coordinate convention used throughout: the deck is a 31 ft (X axis, 372 in) by
21 ft (Y axis, 252 in) rectangle. **Origin (0,0) is the southwest corner.** Decking runs
in the X direction; joists run in the Y direction; beams run in the X direction.

## 4.1 Framing concept

* Three dropped built-up beams run the 31-ft direction at Y = 2 ft-0 in, 10 ft-6 in,
  and 19 ft-0 in (centerlines).
* Joists (2x8 at 16 in o.c.) run the 21-ft direction in **two runs**: each joist line is
  two 10 ft-9 in pieces that lap over the center beam -- exactly what you asked for
  instead of 22-ft lumber.
* Joists cantilever past the two outer beams to carry the rim; beams cantilever past
  the end posts. Both cantilevers are small and code-compliant.
* Five 6x6 posts per beam (15 total) on 15 concrete piers.

## 4.2 Joist design (shown, not just asserted)

Joist span between beam centerlines: 10.5 ft - 2.0 ft = **8 ft-6 in** (both bays
identical by symmetry).

* IRC Table R507.6, SYP No. 2, 40/10 psf, wet service:
  2x8 at 16 in o.c. allows **11 ft-10 in** >> 8 ft-6 in. **OK, with a 39% span margin.**
* Cantilever: the frame is inset 1 1/2 in from the deck edge for the fascia (Sec. 7),
  so the joist tip is at the inside face of the rim, 3 in from the deck edge. Actual
  cantilever from the outer-beam centerline = 24 in - 3 in = **21 in = 1 ft-9 in**.
  Allowable = backspan/4 = 102 in / 4 = 25.5 in = **2 ft-1 1/2 in. OK.**
* Stiffness check (the "modest upgrade" you asked for): the span table is built to
  L/360 at the 11 ft-10 in limit. Deflection scales roughly with (span ratio)^3 for the
  same section, so at 8 ft-6 in this floor performs near **L/(360 x (142/102)^3) ~ L/970**
  -- roughly 2.7x stiffer than a bare-minimum code deck. This is why 16 in o.c. is
  recommended over 12 in o.c. (full comparison in Section 15).
* Why not 2x6 joists? 2x6 at 16 in o.c. allows only 9 ft-0 in (6% margin), gives a
  springy feel, and a 21-in cantilever on a 2x6 is at the edge of good practice.
  Why not 2x10? Nothing to gain -- the beam layout, not the joist, sets the span, and
  2x10 wastes 2 in of the very tight 24-in height budget.

**Joist count:** joist lines at X = 2 1/4 in (end joist), then 16 in, 32 in, ... 368 in
(23 interior lines), then X = 369 3/4 in (end joist) = **25 lines**, each made of two
pieces = **50 joist pieces**, cut 10 ft-9 in from 12-ft stock (the 15-in offcuts become
the beam-line blocking -- Section 10). Each piece runs from
the inside face of a rim (3 in from the deck edge) to 6 in past the center-beam centerline;
the two pieces lap 12 in over the center beam (min. 3 in lap required by R502.6.1; 12 in
provided, offset one joist thickness at alternating sides of the layout line so the
16-in decking-support grid is maintained on both halves).

## 4.3 Beam design

Tributary joist span carried by each beam:

* Center beam B: half of each 8.5-ft bay = 4.25 + 4.25 = **8.5 ft effective joist span**.
* Outer beams A and C: half of one bay + full cantilever = 4.25 + 1.75 = **6.0 ft**.

Design all three beams for the worst case (8.5 ft) so all beams are identical (fewer
mistakes, bulk pricing).

* IRC Table R507.5, SYP No. 2, 3-ply 2x8: allowable beam span is 9 ft-5 in at 8-ft
  joist span and 8 ft-5 in at 10-ft joist span; interpolated at 8.5 ft: approx.
  **8 ft-11 in**. Post spacing used: **7 ft-0 in. OK, 27% margin.**
* Beam cantilever past end posts: posts at X = 1 ft-6 in and 29 ft-6 in; each beam runs
  X = 2 in to 30 ft-10 in (30 ft-8 in long), passing beneath -- and supporting -- the
  end joists at X = 2 1/4 in and 369 3/4 in. Actual cantilever = 18 in - 2 in =
  **1 ft-4 in**. Allowable = span/4 = 84/4 = **1 ft-9 in. OK.**
* Why 3-ply 2x8 rather than 2-ply 2x10 or 3-ply 2x10? (a) A 2x8 beam saves 2 in of
  depth, which this 24-in-tall deck badly needs (Section 4.6); (b) a 3-ply beam is
  4.5 in wide and matches a standard Simpson BCS2-3/6 post cap on a 6x6 with no shims;
  (c) 2x8x16 stock is cheap and always in stock. A 2-ply 2x10 also passes on spans but
  fails (a) and (b).

**Built-up beam fastening schedule (required):** fasten plies with 10d (3 in) hot-dip
galvanized ring-shank nails in **2 rows at 16 in o.c., staggered, from each face**
(outer plies nailed to the middle ply from both sides), plus 3 nails clustered within
6 in of every ply end. Every ply butt joint must land over a post. Ply layout per beam
(splices over posts only):

| Ply | Pieces (cut length) | Splice locations (X) | Buy from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (outer) | 15 ft-4 in + 15 ft-4 in | 15 ft-6 in (center post) | 2 pcs 2x8x16 |
| 2 (middle) | 8 ft-4 in + 14 ft-0 in + 8 ft-4 in | 8 ft-6 in and 22 ft-6 in posts | 2 pcs 2x8x10 + 1 pc 2x8x16 |
| 3 (outer) | 15 ft-4 in + 15 ft-4 in | 15 ft-6 in (center post) | 2 pcs 2x8x16 |

Adjacent plies never splice over the same post. Per beam: 5 pcs 2x8x16 + 2 pcs 2x8x10.
Three beams: **15 pcs 2x8x16 and 6 pcs 2x8x10.**

## 4.4 Posts

* 15 posts: 5 per beam at X = 1 ft-6 in, 8 ft-6 in, 15 ft-6 in, 22 ft-6 in, 29 ft-6 in
  (7 ft-0 in centers), on beam lines Y = 2 ft-0 in, 10 ft-6 in, 19 ft-0 in.
* 6x6 PT SYP ground-contact. IRC R507.4 allows a 6x6 up to 14 ft tall for any deck this
  size -- at roughly 5 in tall, capacity is not remotely in question (a 6x6 SYP No. 2
  short column is good for well over 20,000 lb; max post load here is 2,975 lb).
* **Post height: these are short blocks, cut to fit in the field** (Section 4.6):
  approx. 5 in (Option A decking) or 4 1/2 in (Option B) with the specified 2 1/2-in
  pier reveal. Cut each one individually after the piers are poured so pier tolerance
  never changes the finished 24-in deck height.

## 4.5 Footing design (calculations shown)

Worst-case post load (center beam, interior post):
tributary area = 8.5 ft (joist trib) x 7.0 ft (post spacing) = 59.5 sq ft.
Load = 59.5 x 50 psf = **2,975 lb**.

Outer-beam interior post: 6.0 ft x 7.0 ft = 42.0 sq ft x 50 = **2,100 lb**.
End posts carry about 5/7 of those values.

Required bearing area at 1,500 psf (assumed -- confirm):

* Center beam piers: 2,975 / 1,500 = 1.98 sq ft -> dia = sqrt(4 x 1.98 / pi) = 19.1 in
  -> **use 20 in diameter (5 piers)**.
* Outer beam piers: 2,100 / 1,500 = 1.40 sq ft -> 16.0 in -> **use 18 in diameter
  (10 piers)** (kept uniform along both outer beams for simplicity).

If Blue Springs accepts 2,000 psf, the same math gives 16.5 in and 14 in; you could
then use 18 in and 14-16 in and save roughly 45 bags of concrete. **Ask.**

* Configuration: straight cylindrical pier, full diameter top to bottom, bottom on
  undisturbed soil at **36 in below grade**, top at **2 1/2 in above grade** (total
  height 38 1/2 in). Slight dome/slope on top to shed water. No belling required.
  Cardboard form tubes are optional (only the top 8 in needs forming in cohesive clay).
* Thickness: the pier IS the footing (monolithic column, min 6 in thickness rule far
  exceeded). Plain concrete is code-acceptable for this; two #4 vertical rebar per pier
  is a cheap optional upgrade listed in the materials.
* Concrete: bagged high-strength (4,000 psi) mix, mixed on site as you specified.
  Volume: 20-in piers: area 2.182 sq ft x 3.21 ft = 7.00 cu ft x 5 = 35.0 cu ft.
  18-in piers: 1.767 x 3.21 = 5.67 cu ft x 10 = 56.7 cu ft. Total **91.7 cu ft**.
  At 0.60 cu ft per 80-lb bag: 153 bags; +10% waste = **168 bags of 80-lb mix**
  (about 6.7 tons dry). *Practical note:* that is a very large hand-mix; a rented mixer
  is strongly advised, and a 3.5-cu-yd ready-mix delivery is usually cheaper than 168
  bags -- you specified bagged, so bagged is what is priced, with this flag.

Measured from finished grade (0.00 in), Option A (5/4 decking):

| Layer | Thickness | Elevation (top) |
|---|---|---|
| Pier reveal above grade | 2 1/2 in | 2 1/2 in |
| ABU66Z standoff plate | 1 in | 3 1/2 in |
| 6x6 post block | 5 in (cut to fit) | 8 1/2 in |
| 3-ply 2x8 dropped beam | 7 1/4 in | 15 3/4 in |
| 2x8 joist | 7 1/4 in | 23 in |
| 5/4 decking | 1 in | **24 in exactly** |

Option B (2x6 decking, 1 1/2 in thick): identical except the post block is cut to
4 1/2 in and the joist top sits at 22 1/2 in.

Verification notes:

1. **It fits -- with short post blocks.** A dropped-beam deck at 24 in necessarily has
   stub posts. On a 5-in stub, the ABU66Z base fasteners occupy roughly the bottom
   2 1/2 in and the BCS2-3/6 cap fasteners the top 2 in; the two connectors' screw
   zones just clear each other (offset them in plan a half-inch as well). This was
   checked against Simpson's published connector dimensions -- tight but buildable.
2. If your inspector or an engineer dislikes 5-in stubs, the strongest practical
   alternative is to **delete the post blocks entirely**: pour each pier 5 in taller
   (to elevation 7 1/2 in), cast a 5/8-in galvanized anchor bolt projecting 8 in,
   set the beam on a strip of self-adhering flashing membrane over the pier, drill the
   beam, and countersink a hot-dip washer and nut flush into the beam top. Same
   elevations, one fewer trade-off. It requires accurate anchor placement, which is why
   the stub-post detail is primary.
3. **Ground clearance:** underside of beams is 8 1/2 in above grade and about 5 1/2 in
   above the gravel surface; joist undersides are 15 3/4 in above grade. That is low --
   which is exactly why every framing member is specified ground-contact rated, the
   ground gets fabric + free-draining gravel, and all four sides remain open (no
   skirting) for cross-ventilation. Do not add skirting without adding vents.
4. Pier reveal tolerance: anywhere from 1 1/2 in to 3 in of reveal is fine -- the post
   blocks are cut individually to hit the beam elevation of exactly 8 1/2 in
   (Option A) or 8 in (Option B) above grade. **The deck's 24-in height is locked in by
   the post-block cut, not by the concrete pour.**

## 4.8 Lateral stability of a freestanding 24-in deck (bracing statement)

Diagonal knee braces are geometrically impossible on 5-in posts and are not needed at
this height. Lateral and uplift stability is provided by a continuous load path:
decking screwed to joists (diaphragm) -> H2.5AZ ties and blocking rows -> beams ->
BCS post caps -> posts -> ABU66Z bases -> 5/8-in anchors -> 15 concrete piers totaling
roughly 13,500 lb, plus about 6,500 lb of structure dead weight, all engaged 36 in below
grade. Overturning at a 2-ft height is negligible; sliding is resisted by 15 anchored
piers. Full-depth 2x8 blocking is installed over both outer beams in every joist bay
(48 blocks) to transfer these forces and stop joist roll. This is the standard approach
for low freestanding decks; the IRC's prescriptive deck lateral-load anchors (R507.9.2)
apply to house-attached decks. If the City requests engineering for the lateral system,
have a licensed engineer stamp this detail (Section 17).

# 6. Ground Preparation

Area under deck: 31 ft x 21 ft = **651 sq ft**.

1. **Call 811 (Missouri One Call) before any digging.** Wait for all utility marks.
2. Strip grass/organics 1 in to 2 in across the footprint (deck area only; keep the
   surrounding grade unchanged so the 24-in reference doesn't move). Do this AFTER
   footings are poured and framing is up, or before layout if you prefer working on
   clean ground -- either works; the step list in Section 12 does it after framing so
   spoil from the piers can be hauled out first.
3. Grade flat with a very slight fall (about 1 in over the 21 ft) away from the house
   side so water sheds out from under the deck; compact any disturbed soil.
4. **Landscape fabric:** heavy woven (not cheap spun) geotextile across the entire
   651 sq ft, seams overlapped 12 in, turned up 2 in at each pier, pinned with 6-in
   staples about every 3 ft and at every seam edge. Using 4-ft x 100-ft rolls at 3-ft
   effective width: 7 runs x 31 ft = 217 lineal ft -> **3 rolls** (includes overlap and
   turn-ups; approx. 20% spare).
5. **Gravel:** 3/4-in clean (washed) crushed limestone -- locally called "3/4 clean
   rock." Clean rock drains and discourages weeds; do NOT use minus/fines material
   (holds moisture) under a low deck. Depth: **3 in settled**.
   Quantity: 651 sq ft x 0.25 ft = 162.8 cu ft = 6.03 cu yd; add a 10% allowance for
   settlement/compaction into the fabric = **6.6 cu yd, or about 9.3 tons** at
   1.4 tons/cu yd. Buy bulk from a Blue Springs-area rock yard (bagged equivalent is
   about 360 half-cu-ft bags -- impractical and roughly 3x the cost; both are priced in
   Section 11 for completeness).
6. **Drainage:** the site is flat; clean rock over fabric with the 1-in fall is
   sufficient. Do not create a bathtub -- feather the gravel flush to grade at the deck
   perimeter, never bermed above the surrounding lawn.
7. **Edging/containment:** optional. The deck skirt area is open; if you want a crisp
   gravel edge at the drip line, add steel or composite landscape edging around the
   104-ft perimeter (listed as an optional material). Not structurally required.
8. Spread gravel AFTER piers/posts/beams are set (protects fabric during construction
   and lets you fine-grade around the piers), and BEFORE decking closes off access.
# 7. Decking Layout (both options)

**Critical dimension control:** the FRAME is built 30 ft-9 in x 20 ft-9 in outside of
rims -- 1 1/2 in smaller than the deck on all four sides. The 1 1/2-in-thick 2x10
fascia wraps the frame, and the decking (field, border, breaker) is installed flush
with the fascia face. Result: **finished outside-to-outside decking = exactly
31 ft-0 in x 21 ft-0 in**, with every rim covered. Do not frame to 31 x 21 or the
fascia will push the finished size to 31'-3" x 21'-3".

## 7.1 Board layout math (do this before framing -- it is already done here)

Across the 21-ft (Y) direction, using 5 1/2-in actual-width boards:

* 2 border courses + 43 field courses = 45 courses x 5.5 in = 247.5 in of wood.
* 252 in - 247.5 in = 4.5 in of gap distributed over 44 gaps = **3/32 in per gap**.
* Layout works out to full-width boards everywhere -- **no ripped, awkward final
  course**. That is why the gap is specified at 3/32 in rather than 1/8 in: at 1/8 in
  you would come up 1 3/8 in short at the last course and need a rip. Retail PT boards
  are sold wet and shrink; a 3/32-in installed gap typically opens to about 1/4 in
  after a season, which is ideal for drainage.
* Practical method: snap course lines every 5 19/32 in (5.5 + 3/32); check the running
  total every 5 courses against a tape from the border (should advance 27 31/32 in per
  5 courses) and correct 1/32 in at a time. Board-width variation absorbs the rest.

Along the 31-ft (X) direction:

* Border (5.5 in) + 3/32 gap at each end; breaker board (5.5 in) centered at
  X = 15 ft-6 in with a 3/32 gap each side.
* Each field board run = (372 - 2 x 5.5 - 5.5 - 4 x 3/32) / 2 = **177 9/16 in
  (14 ft-9 9/16 in)** -- cut from 16-ft boards, one clean crosscut each, no butt
  joints in the field. All field joints occur only at the breaker and border, exactly
  as specified. Offcuts (about 14 1/2 in) become spacer gauges and stakes.

## 7.2 Option A -- 5/4x6 pressure-treated decking

  GC is specified here because clearance under boards is low).

  use 5/4 on 24-in spacing.

  a row of full-depth 2x8 blocking at 16 in o.c. (vertically, i.e., along Y) between

* **Center breaker board** runs parallel to the joists at X = 15'-6"; a row of 2x8

# 8. Fascia and Finish Details

* Material: **pressure-treated 2x10 SYP** (1 1/2 in x 9 1/4 in actual), ground-contact,
  selected straight. 2x is specified rather than 1x because it stays flat over the long
  31-ft runs and matches the 1 1/2-in frame inset that preserves the exact 31 x 21
  footprint.
* Coverage: top of fascia tight to the underside of the decking (elevation +23 in,
  Option A), face covering +13 3/4 in to +23 in -- the full rim/end joist plus 2 1/2 in
  of shadow. The decking edge sits flush with the fascia face.
* Lengths: two 31-ft sides (16 ft + 15 ft-1 1/2 in per side, joint centered on a joist
  bay with a 2x8 scab already at the rim splice) and two 21-ft ends (16 ft + 5 ft-3 in
  per end). Corners: outside miters or butt laps (long sides run past the ends). Note
  the fascia corners form the true 31 ft / 21 ft outside dimension -- cut to the
  layout, not to the lumber.
* Fastening: #8 x 2 1/2 in stainless-steel trim-head screws (stainless resists the
  streaking/corrosion that coated screws can show on face work), 2 screws per 16 in,
  1 in from top and bottom edges, predrilled. Leave **1/8-in gaps at butt joints** and
  1/16 in at miters for expansion; keep the bottom edge 5-6 in clear of the gravel.
* **End sealing (required practice, AWPA M4):** brush every field-cut or drilled
  surface of any PT member -- fascia cuts, joist trims, post-block cuts, beam ends,
  countersinks -- with copper naphthenate end-cut preservative (2% Cu, e.g.,
  Copper-Green Brown or clear) before installation. Two coats on end grain.
* No stain or paint is included (per your instructions); if you ever coat it, wait
  until the PT reaches moisture equilibrium (typically 2-6 months).

# 12. Step-by-Step Construction Instructions

Throughout: keep a story pole marked with the key elevations (+2 1/2, +3 1/2, +8 1/2,
+15 3/4, +23, +24 in for Option A) and a stringline reference. The finished elevation
is protected by one rule: **post blocks are cut to measure after the concrete is in**
(Step 8), so nothing before that step can ruin the 24-in height.

**Step 1 -- Permits, utilities, setbacks.** Call Blue Springs Codes Administration
(816-228-0118): confirm permit-or-exempt status (Sec. 2.1 item 3), frost depth, soil
bearing value, footing sizes, and which inspections apply (typically: footing/open-hole
before pour, framing, final). Check your plat for setbacks and easements -- a
freestanding deck usually may not sit in a utility or drainage easement. Call 811 and
wait for utility locates. *Mistake to avoid:* digging before locates or pouring before
an open-hole inspection (if required). *Inspection checkpoint:* written permit answer
filed. *Safety:* none yet.

**Step 2 -- Establish the footprint.** Drive stakes well outside each corner and set up
batter boards. Choose the fixed corner (0,0); run a mason's line for the 31-ft side.
Locate the other three corners at exactly 31'-0" and 21'-0". Mark the FRAME lines too
(1 1/2 in inside the finished lines on all sides). *Measurement:* corners at
(0,0), (31',0), (31',21'), (0,21'). *Mistake:* laying out the frame at 31 x 21 --
the fascia then breaks the exact dimension. *Checkpoint:* self-check. *Safety:* trip
hazards on lines.

**Step 3 -- Square the rectangle (diagonal calculation).**
Diagonal = sqrt(31^2 + 21^2) = sqrt(961 + 441) = sqrt(1402) = 37.443 ft =
**37 ft-5 5/16 in**. Adjust corners until BOTH diagonals read 37'-5 5/16" (within
1/8 in) while the sides stay 31'-0" and 21'-0". Also check a 3-4-5 (or 9-12-15) at the
origin corner. *Mistake:* measuring diagonals to stakes instead of the actual string
intersections. *Checkpoint:* record both diagonals. *Safety:* --

**Step 4 -- Mark footing coordinates.** From the batter-board lines, tape-measure each
footing center from Section 5.2 (X = 1'-6", 8'-6", 15'-6", 22'-6", 29'-6"; Y = 2'-0",
10'-6", 19'-0"). Drop a plumb bob and drive a spike + spray-paint an 18-in or 20-in
circle at each. Double-check the center beam row (Y = 10'-6") -- everything laps there.
*Mistake:* measuring successive 7-ft hops (error accumulates); always pull from the
origin line. *Checkpoint:* 15 marked circles match the table within 1/2 in.

**Step 5 -- Excavate below frost depth.** Dig each hole to 36 in below grade, full
diameter, sides reasonably plumb, bottom undisturbed and flat (hand-clean the last
2 in; do not shovel loose soil back in). A rented one-man auger with an 8-in bit to
break the center, then hand-widen, is realistic in Jackson County clay. If any hole
hits fill, organic soil, or water, stop and call the City/engineer. *Mistake:*
over-digging and "fixing" it with loose backfill -- if you over-dig, fill with
concrete, not soil. *Checkpoint:* City open-hole/footing inspection if required --
schedule by 3 p.m. the prior day (816-228-0104). *Safety:* utility locates current;
keep hole edges clear.

**Step 6 -- Form and pour the piers.** Form the top 8 in of each pier (scrap tube or a
site-built collar) so the top finishes at +2 1/2 in above grade, level across all 15
(check with a line level or laser -- up to 1 in of variation is absorbed later by the
post blocks, but flatter is better). Mix 80-lb bags to a stiff slump; rod each lift to
consolidate; dome the exposed top slightly. If using the optional rebar, set two #4
verticals mid-pour. *Mistake:* soupy mix (weak, segregated); pouring on a disturbed
bottom. *Checkpoint:* pier tops within 1 in of each other; photos of depth before
pour. *Safety:* cement is caustic -- gloves, eye protection; lift bags properly
(13,400 lb total will move through your hands -- pace it, or rent a mixer).

**Step 7 -- Set anchors and post bases.** After 24-48 hr cure, re-string the beam
centerlines and mark each pier center. Drill 5/8-in holes (hammer drill) and install
the Titen HD anchors through the ABU66Z bases; snug, then align each base to the beam
line before final torque. *Mistake:* anchor off-line -- the ABU's slots give about
1/2 in of forgiveness, no more; that is why the string, not the pier, sets the mark.
*Checkpoint:* all 15 bases in line and on the 7-ft grid within 1/4 in.

**Step 8 -- Cut and set the 6x6 post blocks (elevation control).** Set a laser or dry
string at the beam-bottom elevation: **+8 1/2 in (Option A) or +8 in (Option B)**.
Measure from the ABU66Z seat (top of standoff plate) to the line at each pier;
cut each block to ITS measurement (approx. 5 in / 4 1/2 in). Treat cuts, set blocks,
drive SD screws through every base hole. *Mistake:* cutting all 15 to the same length
off one measurement. *Checkpoint:* string across all post tops reads dead level at the
target elevation. *Safety:* cut 6x6 with two passes of a circular saw + handsaw; never
freehand a short block against the fence -- clamp it.

**Step 9 -- Assemble and install the dropped beams.** Build each 3-ply beam ON the
posts or on sawhorses beside them (it weighs roughly 200 lb): crown all plies UP,
splice layout per Section 4.3 (splices over posts, adjacent plies on different posts),
nail per the schedule (2 rows 10d at 16 in o.c. each face, staggered, + 3 nails at each
ply end). Set each beam in its BCS2-3/6Z caps, ends at X = 2 in and 30'-10", and fill
every cap hole with SD screws. Optional: cap beam tops with joist tape now.
*Mistake:* splices between posts (not allowed); crowns mixed up and down.
*Checkpoint:* beam centerlines at Y = 2'-0", 10'-6", 19'-0" (from the FINISHED line);
tops level with each other; caps fully fastened. *Safety:* two people minimum to fly
beam sections.

**Step 10 -- Joists, rims, blocking, bracing rows.** Lay out 16-in centers on beams A
and C from the origin end (first interior line at X = 16 in from the finished corner);
roll out the lower-run joists (tips 3 in shy of the finished edge line = flush with the
future inside face of rim), then the upper run, lapping 12 in over beam B, offset
alternating sides of the line. Nail laps (4x 10d each), toe-nail at beams, install
H2.5AZ at both outer beams on every joist. Cap the joist ends with the rim joists
(30'-9", crowned up, 3x 16d per joist end, splice scabbed); install end joists; then
blocking rows: over beams A and C every bay, lap blocking over beam B, picture-frame
blocking rows at both ends, breaker blocking between X = 176 and 192 in. *Mistake:*
laying out 16-in centers from the frame edge instead of the finished (0,0) corner --
the decking grid and breaker blocking are dimensioned from the finished corner.
*Checkpoint (framing inspection if permitted):* spacing, laps, ties, blocking all per
plan -- schedule before decking. *Safety:* open joist field -- walk the beams, not the
bays.

**Step 11 -- Verify dimension, level, square, elevation.** Frame outside-to-outside
must read 30'-9" x 20'-9"; diagonals of the frame = sqrt(30.75^2 + 20.75^2) =
37.10 ft = **37'-1 3/16"** both ways; joist tops at +23 in (A) / +22 1/2 in (B) at all
four corners and mid-span. Fix anything now -- decking hides everything. *Mistake:*
checking level only at corners; sight the rims for humps too.

**Step 12 -- Ground preparation.** Strip vegetation 1-2 in, fine-grade with the slight
fall, remove all wood scraps and stakes from under the deck (termite bait otherwise).

**Step 13 -- Fabric and gravel.** Roll fabric per Section 6 (12-in overlaps, staples,
turn up at piers), then spread the 3 in of clean rock, raking flush at the perimeter.
Working under 23-in joists is real crawling -- many builders do Steps 12-13 right
after Step 7 instead, before the frame goes up; either order is fine as long as the
fabric isn't torn during framing. *Checkpoint:* no bare fabric showing; rock clear of
post bases by an inch or two.

**Step 14 -- Border and breaker support framing check.** Already framed in Step 10;
now verify the breaker blocking row is centered at X = 15'-6" from the finished corner
and the picture-frame blocking is flush to the joist tops (plane/shim any block that
sits proud -- a proud block telegraphs through the border forever).

**Step 15 -- Deck-board layout.** Measure the actual average board width from your
delivered stack (10-board sample). Re-run the Section 7.1 math if it isn't 5 1/2 in.
Snap the border lines on all four sides at exactly 5 1/2 in in from the finished edge;
snap the breaker centerline at X = 15'-6"; snap a control line every 5 courses across
the joists. *Mistake:* starting the field against a crooked border -- string the
border first.

**Step 16 -- Field decking.** Start at the Y = 0 border line and work toward Y = 21 ft.
Bark side / crown down, best face up; 3/32-in gaps (9/32 at the two borders, Sec. 11.4);
ends cut at 14'-9 9/16" die into the border and breaker gaps; predrill all ends;
2 screws per bearing, 3/4-1 in from edges, screws snugged flush (not buried -- wet PT
craters). Check the running dimension every 5 courses against the control lines and
correct the gap 1/32 in at a time. *Mistake:* letting course lines wander, then
"fixing" with a wedge-shaped board -- the 5-course check prevents it. *Safety:* PT
sawdust -- mask when gang-cutting; wash hands before eating.

**Step 17 -- Center breaker board.** Drop the breaker into its gap over the blocking
row (pieces 16'-0" + 4'-0 13/16", butt centered on a block), predrill, 2 screws per
16 in. Its edges get the same 3/32-in gap as everything else.

**Step 18 -- Picture-frame border.** Fascia goes on FIRST (Step 19) if you want the
border to conceal the fascia's top edge -- recommended order is actually 19 then 18;
they are listed per your sequence, and both orders work because the border overhangs
nothing: install the four border runs to the snapped lines, miters tight and
predrilled, 2 screws at every joist/blocking bearing (16 in o.c.). The border's outside
edge must land exactly on the finished 31 x 21 line -- string it, don't trust the
frame. *Mistake:* screwing the miter tips without pilots (they split).

**Step 19 -- Fascia.** Hang the 2x10 fascia with its top edge tight to the underside of
the decking plane (+23 in / +22 1/2 in) so the border sits flush on top of it; SS trim
screws 2 per 16 in; 1/8-in gaps at butts; corners closed to the 31/21 lines; seal every
cut. (If you follow the recommended 19-before-18 order, tack the fascia 1/16 in high
and let the border push it flush.)

**Step 20 -- Closeout.** Hardware check: every ABU/BCS/H2.5 hole filled, anchors tight,
lap nails in, no shiners at the rims. Re-treat any missed cut. Sweep gaps clear, clear
gravel drips, haul offcuts. Walk the surface: no screw heads proud, no corner rock.
Verify final numbers one last time: 31'-0" x 21'-0" outside decking; +24 in surface;
diagonals 37'-5 5/16". Request the final inspection (816-228-0104, by 3 p.m. the day
before) if a permit was issued, and keep the approval with your house records.

Quantified decking-deflection comparison (relative sag between joists, deflection
scales with span^4 / thickness^3): 5/4 on 12-in centers = 20,736/1.0 = 20,700 (index);

**Recommendation: 16-in on-center.** If you want a firmer walking surface, spend the
upgrade money on 2x6 decking (Option B), not on 12-in joist spacing -- it buys more
stiffness for similar dollars with less labor. This follows your instruction to favor
the practical, economical layout rather than either extreme.
