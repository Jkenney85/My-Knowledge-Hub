# Blue Springs Lawn Action Calendar

Interactive companion: [[Blue Springs Lawn Local Website]]

> [!important] Read this first
> This calendar starts **August 24, 2026** and runs through **August 23, 2027**. It assumes a cool-season lawn based primarily on improved turf-type tall fescue. Product labels are the law; the rates below are planning rates, and the label on the product in hand controls. Do not apply phosphorus, lime, sulfur, or extra potassium unless the soil test calls for it.

## The next 7 days — August 24–30, 2026

### August 24: make the property map

- [ ] Measure every turf section separately. Rectangle: length × width. Triangle: base × height ÷ 2. Circle: radius² × 3.14. Subtract beds, patio, house, and driveway.
- [ ] Name the areas **Front Part Shade**, **Back Heavy Shade**, and any distinctly wet/dry side zones. Do not combine them in the records.
- [ ] Use a 10-foot grid and record: desirable turf %, weeds %, bare soil %, moss %, and dead material %. The five numbers should total roughly 100%.
- [ ] Mark every puddle 30–60 minutes after a hard rain with landscape flags. Return at 4 hours and 24 hours and note which remain.

### August 24–25: retrieve the TruGreen record before treating anything

- [ ] Download or request every 2026 service ticket. Record **date, product name, EPA registration number, active ingredient, and application rate**.
- [ ] If the last service ticket lists prodiamine/Barricade, dithiopyr/Dimension, or pendimethalin/Halts and does not make the reseeding interval clear, call the branch and ask: “What active ingredient and pounds of active ingredient per acre were applied to my address, and on what date?”
- [ ] Do not buy seed until the residual-herbicide decision in [[Blue Springs Lawn Renovation Playbook#Post-TruGreen transition|Post-TruGreen transition]] is cleared.

### August 24–26: identify the grass

Pull ten whole plants with crowns and roots: three from the front, three from the back, two from good turf, and two from poor turf. Do not identify from a mown blade alone.

| Grass | Blade and tip | Collar / ligule | Growth habit | Field clue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Turf-type tall fescue | Medium-wide, stiff; many equal veins; pointed tip; dull underside; rolled in the shoot | Short/indistinct membranous ligule; auricles absent or rudimentary | Mostly upright bunches, no long runners | Individual clumps pull up separately; leaf edge feels slightly rough |
| Kentucky bluegrass | Medium-fine; one strong midvein; **boat-shaped tip**; folded in shoot | Short membranous ligule; no auricles | Spreads underground by rhizomes | Sod holds together; new shoots appear away from parent |
| Perennial ryegrass | Medium; strong midvein plus fine veins; glossy underside; folded in shoot | Short ligule; small clasping auricles | Upright bunch | Shiny back of blade is the quickest clue |
| Fine fescue | Hair-thin, bristle-like, folded; dull | Tiny ligule; no auricles | Bunching or short-rhizomatous, depending on species | Leaves look threadlike and roll between fingers |
| Zoysiagrass | Narrow, stiff, often hairy near collar; hairy ligule | Fringe of hairs | Dense above- and below-ground runners | Tan/straw in winter; very tough mat |
| Bermudagrass | Fine, pointed; often hairy at collar | Fringe of hairs | Aggressive stolons and rhizomes | “Wire” runners root at nodes; invades hot sunny edges |

Penn State’s illustrated identification key is the best companion to this table; it covers vernation, ligules, auricles, and growth habits. [Cool-season turfgrass identification](https://extension.psu.edu/the-cool-season-turfgrasses-identification)

Photograph each sample on white paper with a ruler:

- [ ] whole plant, crown, and roots;
- [ ] top and underside of the blade;
- [ ] blade tip;
- [ ] collar opened to expose ligule/auricles;
- [ ] shoot cross-section showing folded vs. rolled;
- [ ] any runner above ground and any rhizome below ground;
- [ ] seed head, if present.

### August 24–26: complete the sunlight audit

- [ ] At **8 a.m., 10 a.m., noon, 2 p.m., 4 p.m., and 6 p.m.**, photograph the front and back from the same marked position.
- [ ] On a mostly clear day, total actual direct sun for each 10-foot grid square. Bright sky without a sunbeam is not direct sun.
- [ ] Record whether the light is morning, midday, or late-afternoon. Four hours of broken light is weaker than four continuous hours.

Decision:

- **6+ hours direct sun:** improved tall fescue is a sound choice.
- **4–6 hours:** tall fescue is possible but will be thinner; mow 4 inches, reduce nitrogen, reduce traffic, and expect occasional overseeding.
- **2–4 hours:** only a small fine-fescue trial is justified after canopy thinning; do not renovate the entire back as turf.
- **Under 2 hours or dense all-day shade:** use mulch/shade beds/groundcover. Turf is not a responsible recommendation.

K-State defines less than four hours as excessive shade, while Maryland Extension gives tall fescue a four-hour minimum and fine fescue a bare two-hour minimum. I would use the stricter four-hour dividing line in hot, humid Kansas City. [K-State shade guide](https://bookstore.ksre.k-state.edu/download/shade-tolerant-grasses_MF2128) · [Maryland shade guidance](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/growing-grass-shade)

### August 25–27: pull two soil tests

Take **separate samples for front and back** because shade, roots, drainage, and prior treatment differ.

- [ ] Remove surface thatch and living material.
- [ ] Take **12 or more random cores per area** in a zigzag pattern.
- [ ] Established turf: sample 0–4 inches. Bare/renovation soil: sample 0–6 inches.
- [ ] Avoid fertilizer spills, downspouts, compost piles, pet spots, burn piles, road-salt edges, and obvious fill soil. Submit any chronic wet spot as a third separate sample if it looks or feels different.
- [ ] Mix each area in its own clean **plastic** bucket. Do not use galvanized or metal containers.
- [ ] Air-dry overnight, then submit 1.5–2 cups in a labeled box: `Front Part Shade` and `Back Heavy Shade`.

**Primary lab:** University of Missouri Soil and Plant Testing Laboratory, 23 Mumford Hall, Columbia, MO 65211. Request **Package 1: Routine** plus fertilizer and lime recommendation for **cool-season lawn / moderate maintenance**. Current lab pricing is **$15 per sample**, with a **$2 processing fee per sample for fewer than 10 samples**; confirm payment/shipping at submission. Two lawn samples should therefore be about **$34 plus postage**. [MU tests and current fees](https://extension.missouri.edu/programs/soil-and-plant-testing-laboratory/spl-submit-samples/spl-tests-fees) · [MU lawn sampling instructions](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g6954)

**Local handling option:** ask the Jackson County MU Extension office for free sample boxes and whether it is currently forwarding homeowner samples; the actual analysis is still done by MU. **Alternative lab:** K-State’s basic homeowner package is generally about $13–$20 depending on the county office, but use one lab consistently so year-to-year numbers are comparable. [K-State fee example](https://www.douglas.k-state.edu/news-events/soil-test/soil-test-prices.html)

Do not purchase a consumer mail-order “soil health” subscription instead of the university test.

### August 25–28: measure the surface

- [ ] Stretch mason’s line tightly between stakes over each rough section. Use a line level, then measure from string to soil every 2 feet.
- [ ] Lay an 8–10 foot straight 2×4 on edge in multiple directions and measure gaps with a ruler.
- [ ] For broad drainage, rent a rotary laser for half a day. Record elevations on a 10-foot grid and calculate slope: `(high elevation – low elevation) ÷ horizontal run × 100`.
- [ ] Photograph every board/string setup with the ruler visible; photograph puddles at 30 minutes, 4 hours, and 24 hours after rain.
- [ ] Mark utilities before digging or grading: Missouri 811.

Interpretation:

- A depression that is dry after rain and under 1 inch deep is a **topdressing/fill** problem.
- A basin holding water, a reverse grade, water flowing toward the house, or a flat area with no outlet is a **drainage/grading** problem.
- High crowns and bumps are not fixed by pouring material on top; shave/regrade them during renovation or gradually fill the surrounding lows.

### August 27–30: decide front and back independently

| Existing desirable turf | Weeds / bare ground | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 70%+ | Under 30% | Keep it; core-aerate and aggressive overseed only where thin |
| 50–70% | 30–50% | Slit-seed/aggressive overseed after targeted weed control; full kill only if species is wrong or surface must be rebuilt |
| Under 50% | Over 50% | Full renovation is usually more predictable |
| Any % | K-31 clumps, bermuda, nimblewill, quackgrass, incompatible species throughout | Full renovation or targeted total kill of invaded sections |
| Thin surviving turf in deep shade | Exposed soil | **Do not kill the survivors**; correct light/drainage and trial-seed lightly |

MU’s renovation threshold is less than 50% desirable turf. [MU establishment and renovation guide](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g6700)

### August 24–30: baseline photographs and numbers

Create four fixed photo stations with a stake or paint dot. Photograph in the same direction, height, lens, and time of day now; then repeat monthly.

- [ ] Wide front, wide back, and each side yard.
- [ ] Straight-down 3×3-foot quadrat in best, average, and worst locations.
- [ ] Every puddle and drainage path.
- [ ] Closeups of weeds, disease, moss, thatch, roots, and grass ID samples.
- [ ] Mower-height setting and cut grass with ruler.

Record now: exact square feet, direct-sun hours by zone, turf/weed/bare percentages, average mowing time, mower height, 10-can sprinkler output and distribution, infiltration test, soil-test results, high/low elevations, rain date/amount, puddle disappearance time, and annual spending/labor hours.

## 12-month calendar

### August 24–30, 2026 — diagnose and prepare

- [ ] Complete the seven-day baseline above.
- [ ] Order seed only after shade and TruGreen residual checks clear.
- [ ] If a section is a confirmed full renovation, irrigate it every other day for a week if dry, then apply **Compare-N-Save 41% Glyphosate** at the lawn-renovation label rate: **2.5 fl oz concentrate in 1 gallon water per 300 sq ft**. Do not mow seven days before or after; apply above 60°F in calm weather with 24 rain-free hours. Keep spray out of drains and away from exposed tree roots and desirable foliage. Seed may be planted seven days later. [Current product label](https://www.raganandmassey.com/pub/media/files/labels/CNS_84009-27_32oz_web.pdf)
- [ ] If bermudagrass or nimblewill is present, one late-August spray will not reliably eradicate it. Spot-treat now, then plan a repeat next growing season rather than delaying all seeding beyond the good window.
- **Why now:** the prime tall-fescue seeding window is imminent; current condition determines whether fall work succeeds.

### August 29–September 7, 2026 — drainage and rough grading first

- [ ] Extend downspouts to a legal outlet before building drains.
- [ ] Correct broad reverse grades and standing-water basins before seeding. Aim for roughly **1–2% positive surface slope** to a safe outlet and **1–4% away from the foundation** where feasible.
- [ ] For isolated lows deeper than 1 inch, remove sod/vegetation, loosen the clay surface, add compatible screened topsoil in lifts, firm lightly, and final-grade. Do not bury tree flare or add deep soil over major tree roots.
- [ ] For micro-lows only, save topdressing for the seeding sequence below.
- **Why now:** seed cannot compensate for anaerobic soil or a basin that holds water.

### September 1–15, 2026 — primary renovation window

**Front, new seeding:**

- [ ] Mow existing keepers to 2.5–3 inches; collect heavy debris. On a killed renovation, mow dead turf low only after the label wait.
- [ ] Core-aerate compact clay with two passes in crossing directions, producing 2–3 inch cores. For a full renovation that needs final grading, power-rake/verticut only enough to expose soil; do not till wet clay into clods.
- [ ] Final-grade with a 70–80% screened loam/topsoil + 20–30% finished screened compost blend. Apply **¼ inch (0.77 yd³ per 1,000 sq ft)** on established turf; up to **½ inch** only where 75% of the existing blades remain exposed.
- [ ] Seed **Stover New Millennia** or an equivalent blend containing **4th Millennium SRP, Traverse 2 SRP, and Titanium 2LS** at **6–8 lb per 1,000 sq ft on bare soil** or **3–5 lb per 1,000 sq ft when overseeding**. Split the seed in two perpendicular passes. Plant no deeper than ¼ inch. K-State lists all three cultivars for regional use. [K-State tall fescue guide](https://www.riley.k-state.edu/lawn-garden/plants-to-grow/Tall%20Fescue18.pdf)
- [ ] Roll once with an empty/light roller or press with the back of a rake: seed must be in contact with soil but not buried deeply.

**Back, conditional:**

- **4+ hours direct sun:** use the same tall-fescue blend at **5–6 lb per 1,000 sq ft new** or **2.5–3.5 lb per 1,000 sq ft overseed**. Keep surviving shade-adapted turf.
- **2–4 hours after canopy thinning:** seed only a 500–1,000 sq ft trial with a Chewings/strong-creeping-red fine-fescue blend at **3–5 lb per 1,000 sq ft**. Do not use a Poa trivialis-containing “dense shade” mix across the whole yard; it can look good in cool wet weather and collapse or spread into brighter turf in summer.
- **Under 2 hours or wet with no drainage:** no turf seed; convert to a 2–3 inch mulch or shade-bed system after correcting runoff.

**Starter fertilizer:**

- If soil-test phosphorus is low/medium, use **LESCO or Andersons 18-24-12 starter** at **4.2 lb product per 1,000 sq ft** for 0.75 lb N, or up to **5.6 lb product** for 1.0 lb N.
- If phosphorus is already adequate/high, use **24-0-11** at **3.1 lb product per 1,000 sq ft** for 0.75 lb N. Do not add phosphorus merely because the bag says “starter.”
- If results are delayed and the area is bare renovation soil, MU’s fallback is **10 lb of 10-10-10 per 1,000 sq ft**; use that only once and then follow the test. [MU renovation guide](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g6700)

**Germination watering:** use the day-by-day schedule in [[#Watering reference]].

- **Why now:** turf seeded within about a week of Labor Day has far more time to root before winter than October seed.

### September 16–30, 2026 — protect seedlings

- [ ] Keep leaves, traffic, and hoses off soft seedlings.
- [ ] First mow when most seedlings reach **4.5–5 inches**; cut to **3.5 inches** with a sharp blade on dry soil. Never remove more than one-third.
- [ ] After visible germination, reduce watering frequency and increase depth per the establishment schedule.
- [ ] Do not apply blanket broadleaf herbicide. Wait until the new turf has been mowed three times and the exact label permits use.
- [ ] Existing, non-seeded turf may receive a late-September broadleaf spot treatment; new turf may not.
- **Why now:** early mowing stimulates tillering; saturated soil and traffic uproot seedlings.

### October 1–20, 2026 — feed roots, not weeds

- [ ] At about 4 weeks after emergence, apply **1.0 lb actual N per 1,000 sq ft**: **24-0-11 at 4.2 lb product per 1,000 sq ft**. Use a lower 0.5–0.75 lb N rate in the fine-fescue shade trial.
- [ ] Continue mowing tall fescue at **3.5 inches** and back-shade turf at **4 inches**.
- [ ] Water only when rain does not provide about 1 inch/week; use cycle-and-soak on clay.
- [ ] Repair small washouts with the original seed and light compost/topsoil dusting.
- **Why now:** fall nitrogen supports tillering and root growth without the summer disease penalty.

### October 21–November 10, 2026 — leaves and selective weed control

- [ ] Remove or mulch leaves before they mat. In the back, this may be twice weekly.
- [ ] Only after three mowings, spot-treat established broadleaf weeds with **SpeedZone EW at 1.1–1.8 fl oz per 1 gallon water per 1,000 sq ft**; use the lower rate on small weeds/cool conditions and follow the current label. Do not spray fine fescue under stress.
- [ ] Do not aerate, dethatch, or topdress seedlings again this fall.
- **Why now:** perennial broadleaf weeds translocate herbicide to roots in fall, while leaves can smother new turf in days.

### November 1–20, 2026 — late-fall nitrogen

- [ ] After top growth slows but while grass is green and soil is not frozen, apply **1.0 lb N per 1,000 sq ft** using **46-0-0 urea at 2.2 lb product per 1,000 sq ft** or **24-0-11 at 4.2 lb product**. Water it in lightly. Skip or halve this if October growth is still lush, disease is active, or the shade trial already received 1.5–2 lb N total for the year.
- [ ] Keep the final cut at **3–3.5 inches front** and **3.5–4 inches back**; do not scalp for winter.
- [ ] Winterize timers, hoses, and sprayers before a freeze.
- **Why now:** soluble late-fall nitrogen supports winter color and spring root reserves without forcing as much leaf growth.

### December 1–31, 2026 — close and review

- [ ] Clean and dry spreader/sprayer; store herbicides above freezing per label and locked in original containers.
- [ ] Sharpen or replace mower blade; record total seed, fertilizer, water, material, and labor.
- [ ] Compare fixed photos and calculate the new turf/weed/bare percentages.
- [ ] Keep deicing salt off turf; use calcium chloride sparingly where needed.
- **Rate:** no lawn products.

### January 1–31, 2027 — design the next correction

- [ ] Map spring topdressing zones and drainage work from the wet-weather photos.
- [ ] Request two irrigation estimates if the lawn is closer to 10,000 sq ft or if hose watering consumed more than 3–4 hours weekly after establishment.
- [ ] Select shade-bed plants only after confirming summer light and drainage; do not plant invasive English ivy, wintercreeper, or vinca.
- **Rate:** no lawn products.

### February 15–March 15, 2027 — inspect, do not force spring growth

- [ ] Walk only when soil is firm, not thawing and saturated.
- [ ] Count surviving plants in the fixed 3×3-foot quadrats. If fall seed is thin, diagnose light, water, compaction, or residual herbicide before adding seed.
- [ ] Calibrate spreader and sprayer on measured 1,000 sq ft test areas.
- [ ] Do not apply early nitrogen “to green it up.”
- **Rate:** none unless a soil test called for a split lime application; never exceed **50 lb lime per 1,000 sq ft in one pass**.

### March 15–April 10, 2027 — crabgrass trigger, not calendar guessing

- [ ] Measure 1-inch soil temperature mid-morning in three sunny lawn spots. When the 5-day average reaches **50–55°F and is rising**, apply pre-emergent before crabgrass germinates near 60°F.
- [ ] For areas **not being spring-seeded**, apply **Dimension 2EW (dithiopyr) once at 0.37 fl oz per 1,000 sq ft** in the calibrated carrier volume, then water in with roughly **½ inch**. A single label-rate application has a 3-month reseeding interval; verify the current container label.
- [ ] For any area scheduled for spring seeding, skip dithiopyr/prodiamine/pendimethalin. If spring seeding is unavoidable, only a product specifically labeled for use at seeding, such as siduron or mesotrione in an appropriate labeled product, is an option.
- [ ] Begin mowing at **3.5 inches front / 4 inches back** when growth resumes.
- **Why now:** MU recommends application at 50–55°F for five days; crabgrass germination begins around 60°F. [MU soil-temperature timing](https://extension.missouri.edu/news/now-is-the-time-to-apply-spring-preemergent-herbicides-on-lawns)

### April 10–30, 2027 — one light leveling pass

- [ ] Core-aerate only compacted established areas, two crossing passes on moist-not-wet soil.
- [ ] Topdress micro-lows with the compatible loam/compost blend at **¼ inch = 0.77 yd³ per 1,000 sq ft**. Drag/brush it until at least 75% of leaf blade remains exposed.
- [ ] Apply **0.5 lb N per 1,000 sq ft only if turf is pale/thin**: 24-0-11 at **2.1 lb product per 1,000 sq ft**. If fall turf is dense and green, skip spring nitrogen.
- [ ] Do not add topdressing to standing-water basins; fix the outlet/grade instead.
- **Why now:** actively growing cool-season turf can recover before heat, and MU allows spring topdressing as growth begins.

### May 1–20, 2027 — spot control, then stop pushing growth

- [ ] Spot-treat broadleaf weeds only where present. SpeedZone EW: **1.1–1.8 fl oz per gallon per 1,000 sq ft**, per current label. Avoid spraying drought- or heat-stressed turf.
- [ ] Treat escaped young crabgrass with a quinclorac product labeled for tall fescue; calculate from that label rather than improvising a tank mix.
- [ ] Apply **no nitrogen after May 1** unless a turf professional diagnoses a specific deficiency.
- [ ] Maintain **3.5–4 inches** and sharpen the blade after roughly 10 mowing hours.
- **Why now:** late spring nitrogen increases succulent growth, brown patch risk, mowing, and summer water demand. MU explicitly advises against nitrogen past May 1. [MU cool-season calendar](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g6705)

### June 1–30, 2027 — water by wilt, not by weekday

- [ ] Raise front to **4 inches** and keep back/fine fescue at **4 inches**.
- [ ] Start mature-turf irrigation only when footprints remain, blades fold, or color turns blue-gray for more than a day.
- [ ] Supply about **1 inch/week total rain + irrigation**, split into two ½-inch events; use cycle-and-soak if runoff starts. Shade may need less, not more.
- [ ] Scout brown patch after warm humid nights. Correct night watering, excess nitrogen, and prolonged leaf wetness before considering fungicide.
- [ ] Do not aerate or aggressively dethatch.
- **Why now:** taller, deeply rooted turf uses the clay soil reservoir and resists drought. [MU home-lawn watering guide](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g6720)

### July 1–August 15, 2027 — survival mode

- [ ] Mow at **4 inches** only when growth warrants; never mow wilted turf in midday heat.
- [ ] If maintaining green turf, deliver **1–1.25 inches/week** including rain, in early morning. If choosing dormancy, supply **1.5 inches every two weeks** during prolonged drought to protect crowns.
- [ ] Hand-pull or spot-treat only weeds whose label permits treatment in the current temperature; many broadleaf mixes should not be used above about 85–90°F.
- [ ] Photograph disease before applying fungicide. Do not fertilize to “grow out” summer injury.
- [ ] **Do not seed, fertilize with nitrogen, core-aerate, power-rake, verticut, or topdress in July or the first half of August.**
- **Why now:** cool-season turf is under maximum heat, humidity, and disease stress; disruptive work consumes reserves it needs to survive.

### August 15–23, 2027 — prepare Year 2

- [ ] Repeat the sun audit before tree leaves fall and repeat the grid percentages/photo stations.
- [ ] Order the same seed cultivars used in Year 1; check the seed tag for current cultivar names, germination ≥80%, test date within 12 months, and minimal other crop/weed seed.
- [ ] Water dry turf to active growth before any planned glyphosate spot renovation.
- [ ] Mark only the remaining micro-lows for a September ¼-inch pass; send persistent standing-water zones to drainage work, not the topdressing pile.
- [ ] Decide whether Year 2 needs overseeding: seed only if desirable turf is under about 85–90% or bare gaps exceed 4 inches.

## Watering reference

### Calibrate each zone with cans

1. Place 8–10 straight-sided tuna/cat-food cans uniformly across one sprinkler pattern, including edges.
2. Run 15 minutes. Measure each can to the nearest 1/16 inch and average them.
3. Calculate: `minutes needed = target inches ÷ inches caught in 15 minutes × 15`.
4. Distribution check: lowest can ÷ average. Below 0.70 means move/overlap sprinklers before adding more runtime.

Example: average catch is 0.25 inch in 15 minutes. A mature 0.5-inch event takes **30 minutes**. A 0.08-inch germination mist takes about **5 minutes**.

### Day-by-day after seeding

These are starting points; the catch-can result and weather control the actual minutes. The objective is moisture, not a clock.

| Days | Frequency | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Water once immediately after seeding | Wet the upper ½ inch without puddles or seed movement |
| Days 1–3 | 3–4 cycles/day, typically 8 a.m., noon, 3 p.m., 5:30 p.m. | 0.05–0.10 inch/cycle; surface never dries |
| Days 4–7 | 3 cycles/day; add a fourth only in hot wind | Same shallow moisture; no standing water |
| Days 8–10 | 2–3 cycles/day as first seedlings appear | Keep seedbed moist while reducing leaf wetness |
| Days 11–14 | 2 cycles/day | Wet about 1 inch deep |
| Days 15–21 | Once daily, morning | About 0.20–0.25 inch; encourage downward roots |
| Days 22–30 | Every other day | About 0.30–0.40 inch/event |
| After 2–3 mowings | 1–2 events/week | Total 1 inch/week including rain; cycle-and-soak clay |

If water begins to run, stop for 30–60 minutes and resume. In heavy shade, drop a cycle before extending runtime; constantly wet shaded foliage invites disease.

### Practical hose layout for 5,000–10,000 sq ft

- Use one **Orbit B-hyve Gen 2 four-valve manifold/timer bundle** (about $230) at each well-flowing spigot, 5/8-inch hoses, and 4–8 sprinklers left in fixed positions for the establishment month.
- Rectangles: **Dramm ColorStorm oscillating** sprinkler (about $47–$55). Large open/irregular areas: brass impact sprinklers on stable bases. Overlap adjacent patterns head-to-head; do not trust advertised maximum square footage.
- Program outlets sequentially so house pressure is not split. Two independent spigots may run simultaneously only after a bucket-flow test proves pressure remains adequate.
- During germination, automation converts “move a sprinkler four times a day” into a once-daily inspection. Mark each sprinkler base with paint and protect hoses from mower traffic.
- One inch over 1,000 sq ft is **624 gallons**. A green 10,000 sq ft lawn therefore consumes about **6,240 gallons per rainless week**; that is why irrigation becomes worthwhile at the upper end of this lawn size.

## Leveling reference

### Material recommendation

Use a locally screened, weed-free blend of **70–80% loam/silt-loam topsoil and 20–30% fully finished screened leaf compost**, close in texture to the existing soil. Ask for a sample and reject material with stones, woody chunks, odor, weeds, or obvious pure subsoil.

Do **not** use straight sand on KC clay. MU notes that less than roughly 75% sand incorporated through the root zone can worsen a clay mix, and surface sand remains a separate layer. Straight compost improves soil but decomposes and shrinks, so it is not a stable leveling material. A sand/compost sports-turf mix is not the right default over residential clay. [MU soil improvement guidance](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g6955)

### Depth and material table

Formula: `cubic yards = square feet × depth in inches ÷ 324`

| Area | ¼ inch | ½ inch | 1 inch |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 sq ft | 0.77 yd³ | 1.54 yd³ | 3.09 yd³ |
| 5,000 sq ft | 3.86 yd³ | 7.72 yd³ | 15.43 yd³ |
| 7,500 sq ft | 5.79 yd³ | 11.57 yd³ | 23.15 yd³ |
| 10,000 sq ft | 7.72 yd³ | 15.43 yd³ | 30.86 yd³ |

Apply **¼ inch per normal pass**. A ½-inch pass is the upper limit on vigorous established turf only where three-quarters of each blade remains exposed. For 1–2 inch undulations, plan two ¼-inch passes per active-growing year, so full correction commonly takes **2–4 growing seasons**. During full renovation, correct major highs/lows before seed, then use light passes after establishment for settlement.

### Technique

1. Mow ½ inch lower than normal without scalping; bag only the heavy debris.
2. Core-aerate compact clay first.
3. Place small piles on a measured grid.
4. Use a 36-inch leveling rake/lute to cut piles and bridge small lows. Use a landscape rake for edges.
5. Finish with a drag mat or stiff push broom in crossing directions so material falls through the canopy.
6. Stand back at low sun, then check with the 8-foot straightedge. Water lightly to settle; do not roll wet clay heavily.

A leveling rake shapes small areas; a drag mat distributes and works material into turf; a lute is best for final-grade work on bare soil. None of them repairs a drainage basin without an outlet.

## Mowing reference

| Turf / season | Height | Mow when it reaches |
|---|---:|---:|
| New tall fescue first cut | 3.5 in | 4.5–5 in |
| Tall fescue spring/fall | 3.5 in | 5.25 in |
| Tall fescue summer | 4 in | 6 in |
| Shaded tall/fine fescue | 4 in | 6 in |
| Final fall cut | 3–3.5 in front; 3.5–4 in shade | Apply one-third rule |

Never remove more than one-third of the blade. Alternate direction, keep wheels off saturated soil, return normal clippings, and sharpen after about 10 hours of mowing or whenever tips turn white/shredded.

## Soil-temperature trigger guide

| 1-inch soil trend | Action |
|---|---|
| Below 45°F | No crabgrass action; service equipment |
| 45–49°F and rising | Buy/calibrate pre-emergent; confirm no spring seeding |
| **50–55°F average for 5 days** | Apply labeled pre-emergent and water in |
| Near 60°F for several days | Crabgrass germination begins; dithiopyr still has early post-emergent value, but prevention window is closing |
| 65°F+ | Scout and use post-emergent strategy; do not double a pre-emergent dose |

Check the [MU soil-temperature network](http://agebb.missouri.edu/weather/reports/soilTemp2.asp) or [GreenCast map](https://www.greencastonline.com/tools/soil-temperature), then verify your own sunny lawn with a probe. Local soil controls the application.
