# HDX Tote Racks

Product for [[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/WoodWorkingSideHustle - Overview|WoodWorkingSideHustle]], planned 2026-11-05. 2×4 racks that hold **HDX 27 gal Tough Storage Totes four high**. Each tote hangs by its rim on two rails made from a 2×4 ripped in half, and slides in and out like a drawer. Two versions: a **single column** (4 totes) and a **double column** (8 totes). They're for the garage and to sell.

## Design status

Calculated layout only; not yet a physically verified build. The rail spacing follows the usual layout for these totes: a 20-1/2″ opening with rails 17-1/2″ apart. The tote measurements come from the Home Depot listing. The rim height and lid height are estimates, so check them on a real tote before building (see [[#Assumptions to verify]]).

### Decisions

- **Tote:** HDX 27 gal Tough Storage Bin Tote (model 999-27G-HDX). 28.6″ L × 19.6″ W × 15.2″ H with the lid (exterior at the top); 75 lb capacity; 6.1 lb empty.
- **Rails:** 2×4s ripped in half, so each rail is 1-1/2″ wide × about 1-11/16″ tall.
- **Spacing:** about 2-1/4″ between each tote's lid and the bottom of the tote above, for easy loading.
- **Depth:** 23-3/4″, so totes stick out about 4-7/8″ in front and the handles are easy to grab. The rails come from the leftover ends of the 72″ uprights.

## How it goes together

- **Uprights:** 72″ 2×4s with the 3-1/2″ face toward the totes and the 1-1/2″ edge to the front. The single rack has 4; the double has 6, with a front and back pair in the middle.
- **Side frames:** each side is a front and back upright joined by four 23-3/4″ rails, flush with the front and back. The rails do the job of rungs on a ladder, holding the side square. The double rack's middle frame has rails on both faces.
- **Opening:** 20-1/2″ between uprights. The rails stick out 1-1/2″ each, leaving 17-1/2″ between them. The tote body drops between the rails and its 19.6″ rim rests about 1″ on each rail.
- **Rail heights** (top of rail, from the floor): **17″, 34-1/2″, 52″, 69-1/2″**. The bottom tote clears the floor by about 3″.
- **Back stops:** a flat 2×4 across the back edges of the back uprights at each tier, centered on the rail height. It stops the tote's rim so the tote can't slide off the back. It also braces the rack side to side.
- **Bottom boards:** a full 2×4 on edge, 23-3/4″ long, on the inside face of each side frame at the floor. It ties the front and back uprights together at the bottom, flush front and back. The lowest tote's bottom is about 3″ up and much narrower than the opening, so it clears these boards. The double's middle frame gets one board, on either face.
- **Top:** two 2×4s laid flat across the top of the uprights, front and back. The top lid clears them by about 1-1/4″.
- **Size:** single **23-1/2″ wide**, double **45-1/2″ wide**. Both are **23-3/4″ deep and 73-1/2″ tall**.

## Cutting layouts

![[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/Assets/Tote Rack 2x4 Cutting Layouts.svg]]

- **Single: 6 studs. Double: 11 studs. Both: 17 studs.** Buy one extra for a bad board.
- **Bottom boards:** the single rack's 2 bottom boards come from the leftover in pattern C, so it still needs 6 studs. The double needs 3. Two fit on pattern E with its 2 rail blanks, which leaves only 5/8″, so measure first. The third needs one more stud (pattern F), which leaves about 72″ spare.
- Pattern A uses almost the whole stud (72″ + 23-3/4″ + kerf = 95-7/8″). Square one end, cut the upright, then cut the blank from what's left. If a stud is short, cut every rail blank at 23-1/2″; the rack is just 1/4″ shallower.
- **Ripping the rails:** crosscut the 23-3/4″ blanks first, then rip each down the middle on the table saw. Set the fence to about 1-11/16″ (half of 3-1/2″ less the blade kerf) and use a push stick. Short blanks are much easier and safer to rip than a full stud.
- Put each rail's factory edge (the rounded one) on top so the tote rim slides smoothly. Sand the sawn edge.

## Assembly

1. **Check a tote first.** Buy one and confirm the rim is 19.6″ wide and the body just under the rim is narrower than 17-1/2″. Measure how far the lid sits above the rim (estimated 1-1/4″).
2. **Mark the uprights.** Clamp all the uprights together with the ends flush. Mark the rail tops at 17″, 34-1/2″, 52″, and 69-1/2″ from the bottom, and square the lines across.
3. **Build the side frames.** Lay a front and back upright on the floor with the 3-1/2″ faces up and the outside edges 23-3/4″ apart. Screw a rail at each mark, top edge on the line and ends flush with the outside edges, with 2 screws per end (pre-drill near the ends). Then screw a bottom board on edge on the same face, flush with the bottom and with both outside edges, using 2 screws per upright. Check the frame is square. Single rack: build 2. Double rack: build 3, and add rails to the second face of the middle frame (its one bottom board can go on either face). On that face, shift the screws about 1/2″ so they miss the screws from the first side.
4. **Stand the frames up** with the rails facing in. Screw a back stop across the back edges of the back uprights at each tier, centered on the rail top (1-3/4″ above and below the line), ends flush with the outside faces. This sets the 20-1/2″ openings. Use 2 screws per upright.
5. **Top pieces:** lay them flat across the front and back of the upright tops, flush with the outside, and glue and screw them down (2 screws per upright). Check that the front diagonals are equal.
6. **Anchor it to the wall.** A loaded 6 ft rack tips forward easily when a tote is pulled out. Screw the top back piece into at least two wall studs, or use L-brackets.
7. Slide the totes in and check that each one moves freely and stops at the back.

Screws: #9 × 2-1/2″ exterior (the GRK box). About **64 for the single, 112 for the double**.

## Assumptions to verify

- **Tote fit:** the 17-1/2″ gap between rails assumes the tote body just under the rim is narrower than that, which is the usual layout for these totes. Check it on a real tote (step 1) before cutting rails. Totes may vary a little by batch, since HDX says the design may vary by store.
- **Lid and rim heights:** the 2-1/4″ gap between totes and the 1-1/4″ under the top pieces assume the lid sits about 1-1/4″ above the rim and the body hangs about 14″ below it. If the real numbers differ, move the rail marks.
- **Tipping:** the rack must be anchored to the wall. A full single rack holds up to about 325 lb, totes included, mostly high up.
- **Rail strength:** each tote hangs from 4 rail ends with 2 screws each. Fine for 75 lb totes on paper, but load-test one tier before filling the rack.
- **Selling price:** not set. Check local listings for tote racks (with and without totes), then record actual cost and build time.
