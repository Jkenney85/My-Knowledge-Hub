# 2x4 Rolling Workbench

Product for [[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/WoodWorkingSideHustle - Overview|WoodWorkingSideHustle]], planned 2026-11-02. A 2×4 workbench on locking casters with a low shelf, based on screenshots from a video that claims two can be built for about $150 ($40 of 2×4s, $50 of plywood, $60 of casters). Built in pairs from **one 4×8 sheet of 3/4″ MDF**, like the stationary [[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/2x4 MDF Workbench|2x4 MDF Workbench]]: one for the shop and one to sell.

## Is two for ~$150 possible?

The $150 leaves out screws, caster lag screws, glue, and finish. Those add about $90 here, mostly the polyurethane ($49.96), for **about $232.58 for both benches** before tax. Skipping the poly would bring it to about $182.62. That would match the video, but bare MDF swells if it gets wet.

## Design status

Calculated layout only; not yet a physically verified build. The video screenshots show the construction style but no dimensions, so the numbers were worked out to match the stationary bench: same top, height, frames, and MDF sheet layout.

### Decisions

- **Top:** 24″ × 48″, 3/4″ MDF. **Height:** 36″ to the work surface, measured with the casters on.
- **Shelf:** 3/4″ MDF, 24″ × 48″, resting on the bottom frame about 8-1/4″ off the floor, notched around the legs.
- **Casters:** POWERTEC 3″ swivel plate casters with dual locking brakes (8-pack, model 17205-P2NN), four per bench.
- **Finish:** seal every MDF face and edge with water-based polyurethane (2–3 coats).
- **Sheet breakdown:** circular saw at home.

## How it goes together

- **Frames:** two identical 2×4 rectangles, 23-1/2″ × 47-1/2″, boards on edge. Each has two 47-1/2″ rails and three 20-1/2″ cross pieces: one at each end and one centered 23-3/4″ from the end.
- **Legs:** single 2×4s with the 3-1/2″ face to the front. Each sits inside a frame corner, tight against the rail and the end cross piece, and runs from the bottom of the bottom frame to the top of the top frame.
- **Leg length = 35-1/4″ minus the caster height.** The caster height is from the floor to the top of the mounting plate. The listing doesn't give it; the plan assumes **4″**, which makes the legs **31-1/4″**. Measure a caster before cutting the legs.
- **Casters:** each plate (2-9/16″ × 2-3/8″) screws under a corner, 1/4″ in from both outside faces. Its four screws land in the rail, the end cross piece, and the end of the leg.
- **Shelf:** sits on the bottom frame. Notch each corner 5-3/8″ × 3-3/8″, the same as the stationary bench, which leaves about 1/8″ around each leg.
- **Clearance:** about 23-1/2″ between the shelf and the underside of the top frame.

## Cutting layouts

![[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/Assets/Rolling Workbench 2x4 Cutting Layouts.svg]]

- **10 studs** make both benches: pattern A ×4, B ×2, C ×1, D ×2, E ×1. Buy **11** to have a spare.
- Pattern A has less than 1″ to spare. Check the stud length and square one end first. If a stud is short, cut all eight rails at 47-3/8″.
- If the casters measure shorter than 4″, the legs get longer. Three legs still fit on one stud for legs up to about 31-3/4″ (casters 3-1/2″ or taller).

## Assembly (each bench)

1. **Measure a caster** from the floor to the top of the plate, and cut the legs to 35-1/4″ minus that number.
2. **Frames:** glue and screw the three cross pieces between the two rails, with 2 screws through the rail into each end. Square each frame by measuring the diagonals (both should be about 53″).
3. **Shelf:** set the bottom frame right side up on a flat floor and lay the notched shelf on it, finished side up.
4. **Legs:** stand a leg in each corner, through the shelf notch and on the floor, tight against the rail and the end cross piece. Glue, pre-drill, and drive 2 screws through the rail and 2 through the end cross piece into each leg. Hold each leg plumb with a square or a clamped scrap while screwing.
5. **Screw the shelf down** into the bottom frame (pre-drill and countersink, about 10 screws), so it can't slide when the bench is flipped.
6. **Top frame:** lower it over the leg tops so the legs sit in its corners and finish flush with its top edge. Glue and screw as in step 4. Check that the bench is square and the legs are plumb.
7. **Casters:** turn the bench upside down onto a blanket. Place each plate 1/4″ in from both outside faces, pre-drill 5/32″ pilots, and drive four 1/4″ × 1-1/2″ lag screws. Stand the bench up and lock the brakes.
8. **Top:** center it (about 1/4″ overhang), pre-drill and countersink, and screw it to the rails and cross pieces with about 14 screws. Set the heads just below the surface.
9. **Test:** roll it, lock it, and push on it side to side and end to end.

Screws: #9 × 2-1/2″ exterior (the GRK box), about **80 per bench, 160 for two**. Casters: **32** lag screws for two benches.

## Assumptions to verify

- **Caster height:** 4″ is an estimate; the listing gives only the wheel size. Measure before cutting legs.
- **Racking:** each leg is held only by glue and 4 screws at each frame, and a rolling bench gets pushed around. If it sways, add diagonal 2×4 braces at the ends or a 1/4″ hardboard back panel.
- **Caster screws:** one of the four screws per caster goes into end grain (the bottom of the leg). Pre-drill and don't over-tighten. If one loosens, add a 3/4″ MDF or plywood pad under the corner.
- **Load:** the casters are listed at 160 lb each, but the frame hasn't been rated. Don't state a capacity in a listing until it's tested.
- **MDF top span:** with 3 cross pieces, the top is supported every ~21-1/2″. Watch for sag under heavy tools; a 4th cross piece (like the stationary bench) costs about $2.50 more per frame.
- **Selling price:** not set. Check local listings for rolling workbenches, then record actual cost and build time.
