# 2x4 Rolling Workbench

Product for [[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/WoodWorkingSideHustle - Overview|WoodWorkingSideHustle]], planned 2026-11-02 and redesigned 2026-11-05. A 2×4 workbench on locking casters with a low shelf, built the same way as the stationary [[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/2x4 MDF Workbench|2x4 MDF Workbench]]: laminated, notched 2×4 legs, two 2×4 frames with four cross pieces, and a notched MDF shelf. Built in pairs from **one 4×8 sheet of 3/4″ MDF**: one for the shop and one to sell.

The idea came from a video claiming two rolling benches can be built for about $150 ($40 of 2×4s, $50 of plywood, $60 of casters).

The laminated legs and four-cross-piece frames take 3 more studs than the video's single-leg build (which came to $142.47 for these three items). The $150 also leaves out screws, caster lag screws, glue, and finish. With those, it's **about $256.00 for both benches** before tax, or about $206.04 without the polyurethane.

## Design status

Calculated layout only; not yet a physically verified build. It reuses the stationary bench's geometry (top, frames, leg construction, shelf notch, MDF sheet layout) and replaces the foot blocks with casters.

### Decisions

- **Top:** 24″ × 48″, 3/4″ MDF. **Height:** 36″ to the work surface, measured with the casters on.
- **Legs:** like the stationary bench, two 2×4s glued face to face (3″ × 3-1/2″) with a notch for each frame's rail.
- **Shelf:** 3/4″ MDF, 24″ × 48″, resting on the bottom frame about 8-1/4″ off the floor. Notched 5-3/8″ × 3-3/8″ around the legs.
- **Casters:** POWERTEC 3″ swivel plate casters with dual locking brakes (8-pack, model 17205-P2NN), four per bench, under the corners of the bottom frame.
- **Finish:** seal every MDF face and edge with water-based polyurethane (2–3 coats).

## How it goes together

- **Frames:** two identical 2×4 rectangles, 23-1/2″ × 47-1/2″, boards on edge. Each has two 47-1/2″ rails and four 20-1/2″ cross pieces: one at each end and two inside, centered 16-1/16″ from each end.
- **Legs:** each leg is a full-length inner board with a 24-1/4″ spacer glued to its face, leaving a 3-1/2″ notch at each end. Both notches are open at the ends. The finished leg is 3″ × 3-1/2″.
- **Leg length = 35-1/4″ minus the caster height**, measured from the floor to the top of the mounting plate. The listing doesn't give the caster height; the plan assumes **4″**, so the inner board is **31-1/4″** and the spacer is **24-1/4″** (inner board minus 7″). Measure a caster before cutting.
- **Leg position:** the same as the stationary bench. The legs tuck into the frame corners with the inner board against the end cross piece. The long rails sit in the notches, flush with the leg's outer face, and the legs are set in 1-1/2″ from the ends.
- **Top frame:** flush with the leg tops, at 35-1/4″ off the floor. The MDF top brings the bench to 36″.
- **Bottom frame:** flush with the leg bottoms. The casters screw under its corners, and the shelf sits on top of it at about 8-1/4″. That leaves about 23-1/2″ of clear height under the top frame.
- **Casters:** each plate (2-9/16″ × 2-3/8″) goes under a corner, 1/4″ in from both outside faces. Its four screws land in the rail, the end cross piece, and the end of the leg's inner board.

## Cutting layouts

![[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/Assets/Rolling Workbench 2x4 Cutting Layouts.svg]]

- **13 studs** make both benches: pattern A ×4, B ×2, C ×1, D ×2, E ×3, F ×1. That leaves one spare cross piece. Buy **14** so there's a spare stud.
- Pattern A has less than 1″ to spare. Check the stud length and square one end first. If a stud is short, cut all eight rails at 47-3/8″.
- If the casters are shorter than 4″, the legs get longer. Three inner boards still fit on one stud for lengths up to about 31-3/4″ (casters 3-1/2″ or taller). Patterns C, D and F have room for slightly longer spacers too.

## Assembly (each bench)

1. **Measure a caster** from the floor to the top of the plate. Cut the inner boards to 35-1/4″ minus that, and the spacers 7″ shorter than the inner boards.
2. **Legs:** glue each spacer to an inner board, 3-1/2″ from the bottom end, leaving a 3-1/2″ notch at each end. Use a 3-1/2″ offcut as a gauge. Drive 4 screws from the inner board's back face into the spacer so the heads don't show from the front. Make 4.
3. **Frames:** glue and screw the four cross pieces between the two rails, with 2 screws through the rail into each end. Square each frame by measuring the diagonals; both should be about 53″.
4. **Shelf:** set the bottom frame right side up on a flat floor and lay the notched shelf on it, finished side up.
5. **Legs to bottom frame:** stand a leg in each corner, through the shelf notch, with the rail in the bottom notch and the inner board tight against the end cross piece. The leg bottom should be flush with the bottom of the frame. Glue the notch, pre-drill, and drive 2 screws through the rail into the inner board and 2 through the end cross piece into the leg. Hold each leg plumb with a square or a clamped scrap.
6. **Screw the shelf down** into the bottom frame (pre-drill and countersink, about 12 screws), so it can't slide when the bench is flipped.
7. **Top frame:** lower it onto the leg tops so the rails drop into the top notches and the inner boards sit against the end cross pieces. Glue and screw as in step 5. Check that the bench is square and the legs are plumb.
8. **Casters:** turn the bench upside down onto a blanket. Place each plate 1/4″ in from both outside faces, pre-drill 5/32″ pilots, and drive four 1/4″ × 1-1/2″ lag screws. Stand the bench up and lock the brakes.
9. **Top:** center it (about 1/4″ overhang), pre-drill and countersink, and screw it into the rails and cross pieces with about 16 screws. Set the heads just below the surface.
10. **Test:** roll it, lock it, and push on it side to side and end to end.

Screws: #9 × 2-1/2″ exterior (the GRK box), about **108 per bench, 216 for two**. Casters: **32** lag screws for two benches.

## Assumptions to verify

- **Caster height:** 4″ is an estimate; the listing gives only the wheel size. Measure before cutting legs.
- **Racking:** the notched, laminated legs are much stiffer than single 2×4 legs. Still push-test the finished bench, since a rolling bench gets shoved around. Add diagonal braces at the ends if it sways.
- **Caster screws:** one of the four screws per caster goes into end grain (the bottom of the leg). Pre-drill and don't over-tighten. If one loosens, add a 3/4″ MDF or plywood pad under the corner.
- **Load:** the casters are listed at 160 lb each, but the frame hasn't been rated. Don't state a capacity in a listing until it's tested.
- **Selling price:** not set. Check local listings for rolling workbenches, then record actual cost and build time.
