# 2x4 MDF Workbench

Product for [[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/WoodWorkingSideHustle - Overview|WoodWorkingSideHustle]], planned 2026-11-02. A 2×4 workbench with a lower shelf, based on two photos of a GrantMadeThis.com bench (2×4 frames, laminated notched legs, plywood top and shelf). Two benches are built together from **one 4×8 sheet of 3/4″ MDF**: one for the shop and one to sell.

## Design status

Calculated layout only; not yet a physically verified build. The photos show the construction style but no dimensions, so every number here was worked out from scratch around a 24″ × 48″ top and a 36″ work height.

### Decisions

- **Top:** 24″ × 48″, 3/4″ MDF.
- **Height:** 36″ to the work surface.
- **Shelf:** also 3/4″ MDF, 24″ × 48″ and notched around the legs. One sheet gives two tops and two shelves.
- **Finish:** seal every MDF face and edge with water-based polyurethane (2–3 coats). Bare MDF swells if it gets wet.
- **Sheet breakdown:** circular saw at home.
- **Frame:** plain pine 2×4 studs, glue and screws.

## How it goes together

- **Frames:** two identical 2×4 rectangles, 23-1/2″ × 47-1/2″. Each one has two 47-1/2″ rails with four 20-1/2″ cross pieces between them: one at each end and two inside, centered 16-1/16″ from each end. All boards stand on edge (3-1/2″ tall).
- **Legs:** each leg is a full-length 35-1/4″ inner board with a 6″ foot block and a 22-1/4″ spacer glued to its face. This leaves two 3-1/2″ notches: one at the top and one 6″ up from the floor. The finished leg is 3″ × 3-1/2″.
- **Leg position:** the legs tuck into the frame corners. The inner board sits against the inside of the end cross piece, and the long rails drop into the notches so they're flush with the leg's outer face. From the end, the legs are set in 1-1/2″.
- **Top frame:** its top edge sits at 35-1/4″, flush with the leg tops. The 3/4″ MDF top brings the bench to 36″.
- **Lower frame:** 6″ to 9-1/2″ off the floor. The shelf sits on it at 10-1/4″, leaving 21-1/2″ of clear height under the top frame.
- **Overhang:** the 24″ × 48″ MDF overhangs the frames by about 1/4″ all around, so small errors in the frame don't show.

## Cutting layouts

![[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/Assets/Workbench 2x4 Cutting Layouts.svg]]

- **13 studs** make both benches: pattern A ×4, B ×4, C ×2, D ×3. Buy **14** so there's a spare for a bowed or badly knotted board.
- Pattern A leaves less than 1″ to spare. Check that the stud is a full 96″ and square one end before cutting. If a stud is short, cut all eight rails at 47-3/8″; the top still overhangs.
- Cut matching parts in one session with a stop block so every rail, cross piece and leg part is the same length.
- Pick straight studs at the store and keep large knots out of the screw spots.

![[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/Assets/Workbench MDF Sheet Layout.svg]]

The ULTRASTOCK sheet is actually **49″ × 97″**, so all four panels can be cut to a true 24″ × 48″.

1. Lay the sheet on a 2″ rigid foam insulation board on the floor so every piece stays supported. Set the blade about 1/4″ deeper than the MDF.
2. **Cut 1:** crosscut 48″ from the left factory end, using a clamped straightedge.
3. **Cut 2:** trim the right half to 48″, which takes off a 7/8″ strip.
4. **Cut 3:** on each half, rip 24″ from the bottom factory edge. These become the tops.
5. **Cut 4:** trim each remaining piece to 24″, taking off a 7/8″ strip. These become the shelves.
6. Notch the shelf corners (5-3/8″ × 3-3/8″). Run the circular saw to the line on both cuts and finish the corner with a jigsaw or handsaw.
7. Ease the edges slightly with 120-grit sandpaper.

- The full sheet weighs roughly 90–100 lb (estimate). Get help moving it, or have it delivered.
- MDF dust is very fine: wear a dust mask or respirator, and cut outside or with a vacuum on the saw.

## Finishing the MDF

Do this before assembly, after the shelves are notched.

1. Sand lightly and wipe off the dust.
2. Seal the edges first. MDF edges soak up finish, so give them an extra coat.
3. Roll or brush 2–3 coats of water-based polyurethane on every face and edge. Sand lightly with 220 grit after the first coat, because water-based finish raises the fibers.
4. Let the panels dry flat on stickers. Finish both faces so the panels don't cup.

The 2×4 frame can stay bare for indoor or garage use.

## Assembly (each bench)

1. **Legs:** glue the foot block flush with the bottom of an inner board. Glue the spacer 9-1/2″ up from the bottom, using a 3-1/2″ offcut as a gauge for the notch. Drive the screws from the inner board's back face into the foot block and spacer (2 into the foot, 4 into the spacer) so the heads don't show from the front. Make 4.
2. **Top frame:** glue and screw the 4 cross pieces between the 2 rails, with 2 screws through the rail into each end. Square it by measuring the diagonals; both should be about 53″.
3. **Legs to top frame:** lay the top frame upside down on a flat floor and stand a leg in each corner, top end down. The rail sits in the top notch, and the inner board is tight against the end cross piece. Glue the notch and drive 2 screws through the rail into the inner board and 2 through the end cross piece into the leg (pre-drill).
4. **Shelf in now:** with the bench still upside down, slide the notched shelf panel down over the legs, finished top face toward the floor, and let it rest on the top frame. **It can't go in once the lower frame is on.**
5. **Lower frame, built in place:** glue and screw a rail into each pair of lower notches (2 screws per leg). Then fit the 4 cross pieces between the rails: the two end pieces go tight against the leg edges, and the two inside pieces are centered 16-1/16″ from each end. Drive 2 screws through each rail into each end of each cross piece, and 2 through each end cross piece into the leg.
6. **Flip:** tip the bench onto its side, slide the shelf down onto the lower frame, then stand the bench upright. Screw the shelf down: pre-drill and countersink, about 12 screws.
7. **Top:** center the top (about 1/4″ overhang all around). Pre-drill and countersink, then screw it down into the rails and cross pieces with about 16 screws. Set the heads just below the surface so they won't catch work.
8. Check for rocking on a flat floor; plane or sand a foot if needed.

Screws: #9 × 2-1/2″ exterior (the GRK screws already bought for the trees) everywhere, including through the MDF. That's about **116 per bench, 232 for two**. Pre-drill near board ends and in MDF.

## Assumptions to verify

- **Stability and racking:** the legs are held only by glued, screwed notch joints at two heights, as in the photos. Push on the finished bench side to side and end to end. Add diagonal braces at the ends if it racks.
- **Load:** no weight rating has been calculated. Don't state a capacity in a listing until it's tested.
- **MDF in a garage:** even sealed, MDF swells if water sits on it. Keep it off a wet floor; the shelf is 10-1/4″ up.
- **Screws in MDF:** coarse screws hold in MDF but strip easily. Pre-drill and don't over-drive.
- **Poly coverage:** whether 2 quarts are enough for 3 coats on 4 panels (about 67 sq ft per coat) depends on how much the MDF soaks up. Check before starting the third coat.
- **Selling price:** not set yet. Check local listings for 2×4 workbenches, then record actual cost and build time.
