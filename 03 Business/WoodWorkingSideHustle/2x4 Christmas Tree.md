# 2x4 Christmas Tree

Seasonal product for [[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/WoodWorkingSideHustle - Overview|WoodWorkingSideHustle]], planned 2026-11-01. A taller version of a "2x4 Christmas Tree DIY" diagram (Wood Craft Blueprints watermark): a stepped A-frame outline with a bottom crossbar, built from plain pine 2x4 studs. Related: [[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/Cedar Christmas Tree|Herringbone picket tree]].

## Design status

Calculated layout only; not yet a physically verified build. The original diagram is 35″ tall with 12–14″ side boards. The geometry here was rebuilt from scratch and checked against it: using 13″ boards, the model reproduces a 35″ tall tree with a ~21″ crossbar (the diagram says ~20-7/8″). Two taller sizes are drawn: **4 ft** and **6 ft**.

### Changes from the original diagram

- **Taller:** 18″ boards and 3 tiers per side for the 4 ft tree; 21″ boards and 4 tiers per side for the 6 ft tree (a 4th tier keeps the stepped look from getting stretched).
- **Stand:** the original has one 12″ foot in line with the tree, which won't hold a 4–6 ft frame that is only 3-1/2″ deep. Both sizes use **two feet running front-to-back** under the crossbar instead.
- **Joinery:** glue plus exterior screws at every joint (counts below).

## How it goes together

- **Board orientation:** every frame board stands on edge, so the 1-1/2″ face shows from the front and the tree is 3-1/2″ deep.
- **Lean:** each side leans 22.5° from vertical (45° at the peak).
- **Peak:** the left (peak) board runs to the point; the right top board butts into its inside face. Both top ends are cut at 45°.
- **Tiers:** each lower tier sits just inside the tier above it, so the upper tier's bottom end juts out about 1-5/8″ like a branch. Neighboring tiers overlap vertically (2-1/2″ on the 4 ft, 3″ on the 6 ft), face-to-face; that overlap is the glue-and-screw joint.
- **Tier ends:** cut at 22.5° so they sit level once installed. Tier boards are parallelograms: both 22.5° cuts lean the same way.
- **Base:** the bottom tier boards stand on the crossbar. The crossbar ends are cut at 22.5° so they continue the outside line of the tree. Two feet (2×4 laid flat) run front-to-back under the crossbar, centered about 3-1/2″ in from each end of its top edge.

## Cutting layouts

![[03 Business/WoodWorkingSideHustle/Assets/2x4 Tree Cutting Layouts.svg]]

- The angles run across the 1-1/2″ edge (the face you see from the front). Easiest on the miter saw: lay the board flat and set the **bevel** to 22.5° or 45°. Standing the board on edge against the fence with a miter setting also works if the saw clears 3-1/2″.
- Tier boards nest: cut them one after another with the same 22.5° setting so each cut makes the end of one piece and the start of the next.
- 6 ft board 1 has only about 2″ to spare after the crossbar and two tiers. If a cut goes wrong, move a tier to board 3, which has about 11-1/2″ left over.
- Measure the actual stock before cutting. All numbers assume a true 1-1/2″ × 3-1/2″ board. Framing studs have rounded edges and can be bowed, twisted, or knotty; pick the straightest boards at the store and keep large knots out of the angled cuts and screw spots.

## Assembly

1. Cut all pieces; ease sharp edges.
2. Dry-fit the frame flat on the floor, face down. Check that tier overlaps sit tight and both sides match.
3. Tier overlaps: glue, then drive 2 screws from the inside face of each lower tier into the tier above it.
4. Peak: glue, pre-drill, and drive 2 screws through the peak board into the end of the right top board. A 3″ screw grips end grain better here.
5. Base: set the bottom tier boards on the crossbar; glue and drive 1 screw per board up through the crossbar into the board end (pre-drill).
6. Feet: center each foot front-to-back under the crossbar, its center about 3-1/2″ in from the end of the crossbar's top edge; glue and drive 2 screws down through the crossbar into each foot, clear of the tier boards.
7. Stand it up and check for rocking; sand, then paint or stain. Untreated pine needs a finish (and a sealer for outdoor use) or it will gray and rot; the finish is still to be chosen.

Screw count (#9 × 2-1/2″ exterior): 18 for the 4 ft tree, 22 for the 6 ft tree.

## Assumptions to verify

- **Stability:** the frame is 3-1/2″ deep; the feet give 16″ or 20″ front-to-back. Indoors that should be steady; outdoors in wind, a 6 ft tree may need stakes, a weight on each foot, or a tie-back. Test before selling for outdoor use.
- **Peak and base joints** go into end grain. Glue and pre-drilled screws should be enough for a decoration; pocket screws are an option if a pocket-hole jig is on hand.
- **Feet spacing** (centers about 3-1/2″ in from each end of the crossbar top edge) is a calculated position; check that screws clear the tier boards on the real build.
- **Finish:** pine must be painted or stained; outdoor trees also need a sealer. Product and cost not chosen yet.
- **Price:** no selling price set yet. Check local comparables and record hands-on build time.
