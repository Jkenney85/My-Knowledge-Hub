## Primary objective

The purpose of a cold call is to book a free 30-minute discovery call. It is not to sell a website package during the first conversation.

A successful call ends with one of these clear outcomes:

- A discovery call is scheduled.
- The prospect asks for a booking link or follow-up information.
- A specific follow-up date is agreed upon.
- The prospect declines and is respectfully removed from outreach.

## Rules for using these scripts

- Sound like a person, not someone reading a script word for word.
- Be friendly, concise, local, and professional.
- Mention the Blue Springs connection lightly when relevant.
- Ask permission to continue before making the pitch.
- Use one truthful observation from pre-call research.
- Ask questions and listen instead of dominating the conversation.
- Do not insult the prospect's current website or whoever built it.
- Do not make promises about leads, revenue, search rankings, or results.
- Share pricing only when asked.
- Respect a clear no immediately.

## Pre-call research checklist

Spend a few minutes reviewing the business before calling.

- [ ] Confirm the business name and pronunciation.
- [ ] Find the owner or likely website decision-maker when possible.
- [ ] Confirm the phone number is publicly listed for business contact.
- [ ] Check whether the business has a website.
- [ ] Review the website on both desktop and mobile.
- [ ] Test the main contact path or call to action without submitting fake information.
- [ ] Check whether important information is easy to find.
- [ ] Review the Google Business Profile and social presence.
- [ ] Write down one honest observation or opportunity.
- [ ] Decide which script path fits: no website or existing website.

### Useful observations

- The business does not appear to have a website.
- The website is difficult to use on a phone.
- The contact or quote path is hard to find.
- Important services, hours, or location details are missing or outdated.
- The website does not clearly explain the next step for a customer.
- The business relies entirely on social media for its online presence.
- Booking or quote requests require unnecessary phone back-and-forth.

Only mention something you personally verified. Avoid vague claims such as “your SEO is bad” or “you are losing customers.”

## Core cold-call structure

1. Reach the correct decision-maker.
2. Introduce yourself and JKWebCo.
3. Ask for 30 seconds.
4. Share the reason for the call and one relevant observation.
5. Ask one or two discovery questions.
6. Briefly explain how JKWebCo helps.
7. Invite the prospect to a free 30-minute discovery call.
8. Schedule it directly or send the booking link.

## Main cold-call script

### Opening

> Hi, is this [Name]?
>
> Hi [Name], this is James with JKWebCo. I run a local website business here in Blue Springs. This is a sales call, but I did take a quick look at [Business name] before reaching out. Did I catch you with 30 seconds so I can explain why I'm calling?

### If they say yes

Choose the appropriate path below.

### If they say now is a bad time

> No problem. Would there be a better time for a quick call, or would you prefer that I email you a short note and my discovery-call link?

If they do not want either, thank them and end the call.

## Path A: Business with no website

> I was looking at [Business name] online and couldn't find a dedicated website. I did see [Google Business Profile, Facebook page, directory listing, or other presence], so customers can find some information, but there doesn't seem to be one place you fully control where they can understand the business and take the next step.
>
> Is a website something you've considered, or has it just not been a priority?

Listen to the answer, then use one follow-up question:

- How are new customers finding you today?
- What do people usually need to know before they call or visit?
- Would you want a website mainly to generate calls, quote requests, bookings, or something else?
- Has time, cost, or the technical side been the main obstacle?

### Transition to JKWebCo

> That makes sense. JKWebCo is built for businesses that don't have the time or technical interest to manage all of this themselves. I build the site, get it launched, and can handle the hosting, maintenance, and updates afterward.

### Discovery-call ask

> I don't want to turn a cold call into a full sales presentation. Would you be open to a free 30-minute discovery call where we could talk through what a useful website for [Business name] would actually need?

## Path B: Business with an existing website

> I took a quick look at [website address]. I noticed [one specific, factual observation]. I work with small and medium-sized businesses that need a website to be useful for customers without having to manage the technical side themselves.
>
> Are you happy with what the current website is doing for the business?

Listen to the answer, then use one follow-up question:

- What do you wish the website did better?
- Is it easy for you to get information changed when needed?
- Are customers using it to call, request quotes, or book appointments?
- When was the last time the website was meaningfully updated?
- Is there anything about the site that regularly causes frustration?

### Transition to JKWebCo

> That's the kind of problem JKWebCo is designed to handle. I build practical business websites and can also manage the hosting, maintenance, and updates, so the owner doesn't have to chase multiple providers or learn the technical side.

### Discovery-call ask

> Would you be open to a free 30-minute discovery call? We could look at what you have now, what is worth keeping, and whether changing anything would actually make sense.

## Booking the discovery call

### Schedule during the call

> Great. I can schedule that now. Is [Option 1] or [Option 2] better for you?

After they choose:

> Perfect. What is the best email address for the calendar invitation and short questionnaire?

### Send a scheduling link

> Absolutely. What is the best email address for you? I'll send a short note with the scheduling link so you can choose a time that works.

### Confirm expectations

> The call is free and lasts about 30 minutes. We'll focus on your business goals and what the website would need. There is no obligation to move forward.

## Gatekeeper scripts

### Ask for the decision-maker

> Hi, my name is James with JKWebCo. I'm calling about [Business name]'s website and online customer experience. Who would be the best person to speak with about that?

### If they ask what the call is about

> I run a local website business in Blue Springs. I noticed [brief factual observation], and I wanted to see whether improving the website is something the owner or manager is considering. This is a sales call, not a support issue.

### If the decision-maker is unavailable

> No problem. When is usually the best time to reach [Name]? If it's easier, I can also send a brief email for them to review.

### If they will only take a message

> Please let [Name] know James from JKWebCo called after reviewing [Business name]'s online presence. My number is [phone number], and the website is jkwebco.com. I can also send a short email if you can share the best business address.

Do not pretend to know the owner, imply an existing relationship, or disguise the sales purpose.

A standalone website without ongoing service is custom-quoted at a higher rate. The discovery call helps confirm which option, if any, actually fits.

## Objection responses

Use these as guides. Acknowledge the objection, respond once, and avoid arguing.

### “I'm not interested.”

> Understood. Thanks for being direct. I'll mark that down and won't continue following up. Have a good day.

### “We already have someone.”

> That's good to hear. I'm not looking to disrupt a relationship that is working. If you ever need a second opinion or your needs change, JKWebCo is at jkwebco.com. Thanks for your time.

Optional question only if they sound open:

> Are you completely covered for both website updates and hosting support?

### “We don't need a website.”

> That may be true, especially if your current referral and social channels are doing the job. If that changes, I'd be happy to talk through it. I appreciate your time.

Do not argue that every business must have a website.

### “It's too expensive.”

> I understand. The fixed package pricing includes the build, and the care plans are designed for businesses that also want the hosting and ongoing work handled. If that model isn't a fit, there is no pressure. The discovery call can determine whether a smaller scope makes sense, but I don't want to push you past a comfortable budget.

### “I'm too busy.”

> I understand—that is one reason I keep the first step to 30 minutes and handle the technical side afterward. I can send a booking link so you can choose a time later, or I can leave it here. Which would you prefer?

### “Send me some information.”

> Absolutely. What is the best email address? I'll send a short overview, the website link, and the discovery-call link. Is there one concern or goal I should make sure the email addresses?

### “Just email me.”

> Happy to. What is the best email address, and may I follow up once next week if I don't hear back?

### “We built it ourselves.”

> That makes sense, and if it is working for you, there may be no reason to replace it. My role is useful when the site starts taking too much time or needs functionality you don't want to manage. Is either of those becoming an issue?

### “We get all our business through referrals or Facebook.”

> That's a strong position to be in. A website would only be useful if it helped those referrals verify the business, understand the services, and take the next step. If that is not a problem today, I understand.

### “Can you guarantee leads or Google rankings?”

> No. I can build a clear, technically sound website and offer SEO-related services, but I don't guarantee rankings, traffic, or sales. Those results depend on the market, competition, offer, reputation, and ongoing marketing.

### “I need to talk to my partner or team.”

> Of course. Would it help if I sent a short overview, or should we schedule the discovery call with everyone who needs to be involved?

### “Call me later.”

> Certainly. What day and time would be best? I'll note it so I don't keep calling at inconvenient times.

## Three-attempt outreach cadence

### Attempt 1 — Day 1

- Complete the pre-call review.
- Make the first manual call.
- Leave a voicemail when appropriate.
- Send a personalized email when a valid business email is available.

### Attempt 2 — Day 4 or 5

- Call once at a different reasonable business time.
- Refer briefly to the previous call or email.
- Do not leave the same full voicemail again; keep it short.

### Attempt 3 — Day 10 to 14

- Make one final call or send the final follow-up email.
- State that this is the last follow-up.
- Close the lead unless the prospect asked for later contact.

Stop immediately after a clear decline or do-not-call request.

## Compliance and respectful-outreach checklist

This is an operating checklist, not legal advice. Recheck federal and state requirements before expanding outreach, using automation, hiring callers, recording calls, or contacting personal numbers.

- Call only publicly listed business contact numbers under the current launch process.
- Place calls manually; do not use prerecorded messages, robocalls, spoofed caller ID, or automated dialing.
- Identify yourself, JKWebCo, and the sales purpose honestly near the start.
- Use accurate caller ID information and a working callback number.
- Call during the prospect's reasonable local business hours and avoid known peak periods for that industry.
- Maintain an internal do-not-call list.
- Record and honor any request not to be contacted again.
- Never make misleading claims about an existing relationship, website problems, results, pricing, or urgency.
- Limit the normal sequence to three attempts over roughly two weeks.
- Do not record a call unless the applicable consent laws have been reviewed and satisfied.
- Treat a number as personal when its status is unclear and verify the applicable rules before calling.

Current reference points:

- The FTC states that most business-to-business solicitation calls are exempt from the Telemarketing Sales Rule, but deceptive or misleading B2B telemarketing practices remain prohibited: [FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-telemarketing-sales-rule).
- The National Do Not Call Registry is for personal phone numbers rather than business lines: [FTC National Do Not Call Registry FAQ](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/national-do-not-call-registry-faqs).
- Missouri's No-Call list applies to residential and personal cell numbers, not business phone numbers: [Missouri Attorney General No-Call guidance](https://ago.mo.gov/get-help/no-call/).

Because a publicly advertised number may still be a personal mobile number, the safest launch practice is manual, clearly identified calling, prompt respect for opt-outs, careful recordkeeping, and legal review before scaling.
