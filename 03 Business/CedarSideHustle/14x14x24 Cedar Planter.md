# 14x14x24 Cedar Planter

First planned product for [[03 Business/CedarSideHustle/CedarSideHustle - Overview|CedarSideHustle]].

## Material and dimensions

- **Stock:** 5/8″ thick × 5-1/2″ wide × 72″ long incense cedar dog-ear fence pickets.
- **Original product concept:** 14″ × 14″ × 24″.
- **Dimension decision:** prioritize a five-picket build and retain 14″ side pieces; finished outside width and depth may be larger once the corner legs and rim are included. Final dimensions depend on corner joinery and rim placement and must be verified before advertising a size. The 24″ measurement currently describes the leg stock length.
- **Construction thickness:** all listed pieces retain the picket thickness of 5/8″.

### Saw-cut allowance and assumptions

- Illustrative blade kerf: 1/8″; verify against the actual blade.
- For pickets 1–3, five 14″ parts use 70″. Allowing five crosscuts uses another 5/8″, leaving about 1-3/8″ for an offcut and any end trimming. Keep the dog-ear end in a bottom piece, positioning that piece so the square side pieces avoid the angled corners.
- The wide-piece layout assumes the dog-ear area fits within the designated bottom piece and the stock has enough usable length after any required trimming.
- Each narrow strip requires 62″ of finished lengths. Allowing three crosscuts at 1/8″ leaves about 9-5/8″ before end trimming.
- Rip the two 2″ strips from straight, usable stock. Two 2″ strips plus two 1/8″ rip cuts fit within 5-1/2″, leaving approximately 1-1/4″ of width as an offcut.
- On pickets 4–5, remove or avoid the dog-ear section before producing rectangular narrow parts; the length allowance provides room for this, subject to measuring the actual stock.
- Five pickets is the calculated requirement for sound stock. Defects, splits, excessive trimming, or cutting mistakes can require additional material.
- Confirm bottom fit, support, and drainage on the prototype; the cut list alone does not establish the assembled geometry.

## Confirmed assembly details

- Four L-shaped corner legs, each made from two 2″ × 24″ pieces.
- Twelve full-width side pieces and three full-width bottom pieces.
- The three bottom pieces may retain dog-ear corners.
- Four 2″ × 14″ pieces form the top rim; flat placement and rotating butt joints are proposed below for the prototype.
- Keep lumber usage to five pickets; choose the final assembled dimensions around that constraint.

## Intended use and design requirements

- Primary intended use: hold soil directly, with a liner of some kind.
- A removable pot is also an option; its fit and availability have not been established.
- Design the bottom supports for the intended soil-filled use; verify support, stability, and drainage with the prototype before selling.
- Recommended starter liner: water-permeable landscape fabric to retain potting mix while allowing drainage. Verify the chosen fabric actually drains water; it is not a waterproof barrier protecting cedar from all moisture. A removable pot remains optional and must fit the finished opening.
- Include bottom drainage gaps and test drainage with the liner installed.
- User is loosely following a video without specific dimensions; develop the construction details independently.

## Finish

- **Confirmed first version:** natural unfinished cedar, with no stain or sealer.
- Sand rough surfaces and ease sharp edges before sale.
- Budget no stain/sealer for this version; retain sanding consumables in the cost worksheet.

## Draft assembly geometry

This is a calculated layout for prototype development, not a physically verified build plan. It preserves the original five-picket blank-cutting layout.

![[03 Business/CedarSideHustle/Assets/Planter Corner Layout.png]]

- **Side walls:** use three horizontal 5-1/2″ × 14″ pieces per side. At each corner, one wall end meets the face of the next wall, with the overlap rotating around the box. This repeated corner arrangement allows all twelve side pieces to remain 14″ long and produces a square body approximately 14-5/8″ outside and 13-3/8″ inside, assuming actual 5/8″ stock and no gaps.
- **Legs:** wrap each body corner with two 2″ × 24″ pieces in an L. The two equal-width pieces produce unequal visible leg-face widths at a butt joint; verify the appearance on the prototype. Legs add 5/8″ beyond each body face, giving a maximum footprint of approximately 15-7/8″ before the rim.
- **Top rim:** lay the four 2″ × 14″ pieces flat and use the same rotating butt-joint arrangement. The resulting rim is 16″ square outside, with a 12″ square opening. Center it over the box; this leaves approximately 1/16″ overhang beyond the legs on each side, so actual thickness and alignment matter.
- **Height:** with full 24″ legs and a 5/8″ rim resting on top, finished height is approximately 24-5/8″. Product dimensions would therefore be approximately **16″ × 16″ × 24-5/8″**, subject to prototype verification.
- **Wall position:** align the top of the 16-1/2″-tall wall panels with the tops of the legs, leaving approximately 7-1/2″ of leg below the walls before adding bottom supports.
- **Bottom:** shorten the three 14″ bottom blanks to fit the measured inside span (nominally 13-3/8″). Two full-width boards plus a third ripped to approximately 2-1/8″ fill that width with two 1/8″ drainage gaps. Trim to actual fit; dog-ear openings may remain in bottom pieces if soil is retained by the liner.
- **Bottom supports:** proposed four-sided support frame from the reserved narrow offcuts, installed inside the bottom wall course. Details below; a filled/watered prototype check is required and no supported weight is established yet.
- **Selling dimensions:** use the measured finished prototype dimensions in listings; the existing note title preserves the original concept name only.

### Proposed bottom-support frame

Use the actual leftover strip width, nominally 1-1/4″, standing vertically; the 5/8″ thickness projects inward as the ledge beneath the bottom boards.

| Quantity | Nominal width/height | Length | Position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 1-1/4″ | 13-3/8″ | Opposing inside walls; bottom boards span between these rails |
| 2 | 1-1/4″ | 12-1/8″ | Remaining walls, fitted between the first two rails |

- Cut one long and one short rail from each leftover strip. This uses approximately 25-1/2″ plus kerf per strip and keeps the total at five pickets.
- Align all rail tops. With walls starting 7-1/2″ above the feet and rails starting at the same height, rail tops sit at 8-3/4″ and the 5/8″ floor tops sit at 9-3/8″. These positions are proposed and may change after fitting.
- Orient the L-shaped legs so their faces on the walls carrying the shorter rails cover a full 2″ of wall from each body corner. This gives those rail-end screws room behind the legs despite the inset caused by the longer rails. Check every planned screw position against the dry-fitted layers before assembly.
- Fit the floor with drainage gaps and retain access for water to exit beneath it; do not glue the bottom boards into a solid panel.
- Proposed support fasteners: stainless steel screws driven from inside each rail, through the side wall, into the overlapping corner leg. Locate rail-end fasteners only where all three wood layers are present. Start with one screw at each rail end, eight total, and assess whether additional reinforcement is needed during prototype testing.
- For actual 5/8″ stock, those three layers total 1-7/8″. A nominal 1-3/4″ screw is a candidate, leaving only 1/8″ of nominal tip clearance; measure real thickness, head seating, and screw length before choosing it. Use a shorter fastener if necessary to avoid exposed tips. Fastener diameter, pilot holes, and edge spacing must follow the selected screw's guidance and avoid splitting the narrow cedar.
- This fastener layout is a prototype proposal, not a verified load rating. Check the whole load path from floor to rails, walls, and legs. Reinforce the design if the watered test shows movement, splitting, sagging, or loosening; revise cost if reinforcement requires additional hardware or lumber.

### Prototype assembly sequence

1. Select five sound pickets and measure actual thickness, width, usable length, and blade kerf.
2. From each of pickets 1–3, cut four square 14″ wall pieces from the square end, then recover one 14″ bottom blank containing the dog-ear tip. Check the offcut allowance before cutting; preserve the angled end for the bottom.
3. From each of pickets 4–5, rip two 2″ strips and reserve the remaining narrow strip. Square off the dog-ear area where needed, then cut each 2″ strip into 24″ + 24″ + 14″. Cut the support rails from the reserved strips.
4. Dry-fit the rotating wall corners, L-shaped legs, and flat rim. Measure both diagonals to check that the body and rim are square. Verify the legs fit beneath the rim before gluing.
5. Prepare clean, dry, tight-fitting bonding surfaces. Glue and brad the wall/leg assembly using Titebond III, with clamps or fixtures holding joints tightly together. Keep glue off the edges between stacked wall courses so those boards are not forced into one wide glued panel.
6. Fit the support frame and pilot/drill its mechanical fasteners as described above. Trim the bottom blanks to the measured interior; rip the third bottom piece to leave the intended drainage gaps.
7. Glue/brad the flat top rim, sand rough surfaces, and ease sharp edges. Test brad length and nailer depth on matching offcuts first; no exposed tips are acceptable. A 1″ brad is a starting candidate for two stacked 5/8″ layers, subject to tool compatibility and actual fit. Choose corrosion-resistant fasteners rated for outdoor use; confirm gauge and supported nail lengths from the nailer.
8. Follow Titebond's clamping guidance and wait at least 24 hours before stressing fresh glue joints. Freshly sand cedar bonding surfaces shortly before glue-up, as recommended by Titebond.
9. Fit a permeable liner and test water flow. Measure and weigh the intended potting mix and water load, check floor/support deflection and frame stability over repeated watering, and inspect joints before treating the design as saleable. These observations are practical prototype checks and do not establish a long-term engineering load rating.
10. Record all measured dimensions, material usage, hands-on time, defects/rework, and total cost. Revise the plan before building the saleable batch.

### Liner source

[University of Minnesota Extension: Raised bed gardens, Liners](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/raised-bed-gardens) recommends water-permeable material such as landscape fabric where a bottom liner is needed to retain soil. Applying that drainage principle to this elevated planter is a design recommendation. Reviewed 2026-10-09.
