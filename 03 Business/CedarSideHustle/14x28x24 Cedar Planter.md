# 14x28x24 Cedar Planter

Rectangular product concept for [[03 Business/CedarSideHustle/CedarSideHustle - Overview|CedarSideHustle]], requested 2026-10-09. Related: [[03 Business/CedarSideHustle/14x14x24 Cedar Planter|Square planter]].

## Design status

Initial calculated layout only; not yet a physically verified build plan. User confirmed on 2026-10-09 that dimensions should be treated like the first planter: **14″ short wall pieces, 28″ long wall pieces, and 24″ legs**, allowing the finished outside dimensions to be larger.

User also confirmed **six legs**: four corner legs and two middle supports, one midway along each long side.

Carry forward the current material and product preferences for this draft: 5/8″ × 5-1/2″ × 72″ incense cedar dog-ear pickets at $3.48 each, natural unfinished cedar, three horizontal boards per wall, brads and Titebond III for suitable joints, mechanically fastened bottom supports, and a permeable liner for soil-filled use. The prototype must verify actual dimensions, support performance, costs, and time before sale.

## Eight-picket cutting layout for six legs

This replaces the preliminary seven-picket/four-corner-leg layout. The eighth picket supplies middle-leg parts and taller long bottom rails.

| Pickets | Full-width crosscuts per picket | Intended use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–3 | 28″ + 28″ + 14″ | Six long wall boards total; three dog-ear bottom blanks |
| 4 | Five 14″ pieces | Four short wall boards and one dog-ear bottom blank |
| 5 | 28″ + 14″ + 14″ + 14″ | Rip the square 28″ piece into two 2″-wide long rim pieces; use two square 14″ pieces for short walls and the dog-ear 14″ piece for the bottom |
| 6–7 | Rip two 2″ strips per picket; cut each strip into 24″ + 24″ + 14″ | Eight leg pieces and four short 14″ rim blanks; only two short rim blanks are needed, leaving two spare 2″ × 14″ pieces |
| 8 | Rip two 2″ strips; cut each into 24″ + 27-3/8″ + 7-1/2″ + 7-1/2″ | Two full-height outer middle-leg boards, two long bottom rails, two lower spacers, and two inside bearing feet |

- Keep the dog-ear end in a bottom blank on each of pickets 1–5; wall boards and rim pieces need rectangular ends.
- Use the two spare 2″ × 14″ pieces from pickets 6–7 as inside upper backers for the middle legs; trim each to 13-3/4″ during fitting.
- Cut the two short bottom rails from the approximately 1-1/4″-wide leftover strips on pickets 6–8. Reserve the remaining offcuts, including the long-rim blank remainder, for fitting and possible reinforcement.
- **Lumber requirement:** eight sound pickets for this calculated layout, subject to actual trimming, defects, and prototype revisions.
- **Lumber cost:** 8 × $3.48 = **$27.84 before tax**. This is $3.48 more than the preliminary four-leg rectangular layout and $10.44 more than the square planter's lumber cost.
- This is a feasible cutting layout, not a proven mathematical minimum or an established supported-weight rating.

### Kerf allowance

Illustrative 1/8″ blade kerf, counting one crosscut for each recovered piece:

- 28″ + 28″ + 14″ uses 70-3/8″, leaving 1-5/8″.
- Five 14″ pieces use 70-5/8″, leaving 1-3/8″.
- 28″ + three 14″ pieces uses 70-1/2″, leaving 1-1/2″.
- Each 24″ + 24″ + 14″ narrow strip uses 62-3/8″, leaving 9-5/8″ before dog-ear/end trimming.
- Each picket-8 strip uses 66-7/8″ for 24″ + 27-3/8″ + 7-1/2″ + 7-1/2″ including four cuts, leaving 5-1/8″ for dog-ear/end trimming.
- Two 2″ strips plus two 1/8″ rip cuts use 4-1/4″ of a 5-1/2″ width, leaving approximately 1-1/4″ for a support strip.

Measure the actual stock and kerf before cutting. Bottom blanks deliberately retain the dog-ear corners so the tight wide-board layouts do not depend on removing the tip.

## Draft geometry using the confirmed side-piece lengths

- Rotating butt joints give a body approximately **14-5/8″ × 28-5/8″ outside**, with a **13-3/8″ × 27-3/8″ inside** cavity.
- The four flat 2″-wide rim pieces, using rotating butt joints, form a **16″ × 30″ outside rim** with a **12″ × 26″ opening**.
- Full 24″ legs plus a 5/8″ rim on top give approximately **24-5/8″ finished height**.
- Overall proposed finished dimensions are therefore approximately **16″ × 30″ × 24-5/8″**, subject to prototype measurement and the final support layout.
- Three 5-1/2″ wall boards give the same 16-1/2″ wall height as the square version.
- Keep the middle-leg outer boards a single 5/8″ layer outside the walls. Their other parts sit in the wall's thickness below the box or inside it; this avoids widening the external footprint with a double-thickness outside post.

## Bottom boards and support frame

- Run the bottom boards across the short inside dimension, approximately 13-3/8″, rather than making them span the full 27-3/8″ length.
- Five 14″ bottom blanks can cover the long inside dimension after trimming: four boards at 5-1/2″ wide and a fifth at approximately 4-7/8″ wide, with four 1/8″ gaps, total 27-3/8″. All five are trimmed to the measured short inside span.
- Dog-ear openings must be contained by the liner; test drainage with the actual installed fabric.
- **Long rails:** two 2″ × 27-3/8″ pieces from picket 8, standing vertically against the inside long walls. Their 5/8″ thickness forms the ledge beneath the floor. Use the 2″ height rather than the narrower rip leftovers for these longer rails.
- **Short rails:** two approximately 1-1/4″ × 12-1/8″ pieces, fitted between the long rails at the short ends. Their tops align with the long-rail tops; trim lengths to the measured fit.
- With 24″ legs and 16-1/2″ walls aligned at the top, the walls begin 7-1/2″ above the feet. Put the long-rail bottoms at that same height. The 2″ rails then end at **9-1/2″**, and the 5/8″ floor tops sit at **10-1/8″**.
- Position the middle legs at the midpoints of the long walls. Their inside bearing feet stand directly under the long rails. The intended load path is **floor → long rail → inside bearing foot → ground**, with mechanical fasteners holding the assembly together.
- At equal fill depth, the nominal inside area is about 2.05 times the square prototype's area. This version raises the floor slightly because of its taller rails, so actual capacity depends on the chosen fill height. Measure the intended watered potting mix instead of assigning a weight from dimensions alone.

## Middle-leg assembly: one on each long side

![[03 Business/CedarSideHustle/Assets/Rectangular Planter Six Leg Layout.png]]

Each middle leg uses these parts, all 2″ wide and 5/8″ thick:

| Part | Length | Position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outside board | 24″ | Full height outside the long wall, from feet to underside of rim |
| Lower spacer | 7-1/2″ | Behind the outside board, occupying the wall's thickness below the wall |
| Inside bearing foot | 7-1/2″ | Behind the spacer, extending from the ground to the underside of the long rail |
| Inside upper backer | 13-3/4″ | Inside the wall, from 10-1/4″ above the feet up to 24″ |

### How the pieces fit

- Below the wall, outside board + spacer + bearing foot form a three-layer foot, nominally 1-7/8″ thick and 2″ wide. Glue, clamp, and mechanically fasten this lower sandwich; keep all three bottoms flush.
- Above 7-1/2″, the actual wall replaces the lower spacer. The long rail occupies the inside layer between heights 7-1/2″ and 9-1/2″ and bears directly on the inside foot's top.
- The inside upper backer starts at 10-1/4″, leaving nominal 1/8″ clearance above the floor top at 10-1/8″. It sandwiches the wall against the full-height outside board, helping tie the three wall courses to the middle leg. Trim the backer to actual fit if the floor height changes.
- The backer projects 5/8″ into the cavity. The rim overlaps the cavity by nominally 11/16″ on each long side, covering the backer at the top with only 1/16″ allowance; check actual alignment.
- This assembly provides direct bearing under the rail while retaining the 16″-wide rim footprint. Its proportions and attachment are a prototype design, not a verified engineering rating.

## Prototype fastening plan

- Continue Titebond III and corrosion-resistant brads for suitable wall/leg and rim joints; keep adjacent wall-board edges unglued. Clamp tight-fitting joints and follow the adhesive instructions.
- Use exterior wood screws compatible with cedar for the support frame and middle-leg assemblies. Stainless steel is a preferred corrosion-resistant option. Pilot holes and edge/end spacing must follow the chosen screw's guidance and avoid splitting the thin cedar.
- Candidate length for **three actual 5/8″ layers**: 1-3/4″, leaving nominally 1/8″ to the far face before any countersinking. Verify the real stack thickness, screw length, and head seating with offcuts. Do not use that length in only two layers or allow exposed tips.
- **Long-rail corner ends:** proposed two screws per end, through rail + wall + overlapping corner leg, eight total. Fit the L-shaped corner legs so the intended screws all land in three layers.
- **Short-rail corner ends:** proposed one screw per end, through rail + wall + overlapping corner leg, four total. Verify the thinner rail's edge clearance and placement.
- **Long rail at each middle leg:** proposed two screws, through rail + wall + outside middle-leg board, four total. Keep the rail resting on the bearing foot while fastening.
- **Lower middle-leg sandwich:** proposed two screws per assembly, through inside bearing foot + spacer + outside board, four total. Separate their heights and keep clear of part ends.
- **Upper inside backers:** proposed one screw through each wall course per assembly, through backer + wall + outside board, six total. Locate screws within solid boards, not at the gaps between courses.
- This provisional schedule totals **26 support/middle-leg screws**, excluding any other screws added after the prototype. It is an initial layout to assess for fit and splitting, not a tested fastener capacity.
- Test the brad gauge/length and nailer depth on matching offcuts. A 1″ brad is a candidate for two 5/8″ layers, subject to the tool's compatibility and actual fit; confirm corrosion resistance for outdoor use.

## Prototype build sequence

1. Select eight sound pickets, measure actual dimensions and kerf, and mark the parts using the table above. Preserve the dog-ear ends in the five bottom blanks.
2. Cut wall boards, rip/cut leg and rim parts, and reserve the short-strip leftovers for end rails. From picket 8, cut the long support rails, full-height middle-leg outer boards, and four short foot/spacer pieces.
3. Dry-fit the rotating wall corners, four L-shaped corner legs, and flat rim. Check diagonals for square and measure the actual inside cavity.
4. Prepare clean, dry cedar bonding surfaces. Glue/brad and clamp the wall/corner-leg assembly, keeping all feet level and the wall bottoms at the chosen height.
5. Mark the midpoint of each long side. Assemble the lower sandwiches for the two middle legs, then fit them so the inside bearing feet sit directly under the long rails. Add the proposed mechanical fasteners.
6. Fit the long and short support rails with level tops. Align and fasten them to the corner and middle legs, checking that no screw tips emerge. Fit the upper inside backers after confirming the floor height.
7. Trim bottom blanks to the actual short inside span, narrow the last board to leave four drainage gaps, and dry-fit the floor. Keep it removable for inspection on the prototype; do not glue the floor into a solid panel.
8. Fit the rim, sand rough faces, and ease sharp edges. Follow Titebond's clamping guidance and wait at least 24 hours before stressing fresh glue joints.
9. Fit a water-permeable liner, test drainage, then test using the intended potting mix and watering. Observe the floor, rails, bearing feet, joints, and six-point stability on a flat surface over repeated watering. Correct rocking, gaps beneath bearing feet, splitting, sagging, or loosening before making saleable units.
10. Record actual outside dimensions, material use, hands-on time, filled weight, and any reinforcement required. A practical prototype check does not establish a long-term rated load or guaranteed service life.

## Reference guidance

- [Titebond FAQs](https://www.titebond.com/resources/use/all/faqs): cedar surface preparation, clamping, and waiting at least 24 hours before stressing new joints.
- [Titebond III product details](https://www.titebond.com/product/wood-glues/e8d40b45-0ab3-49f7-8a9c-b53970f736af): exterior suitability and limitations, including structural/load-bearing uses and continuous submersion.
- [Simpson Strong-Tie stainless steel fasteners](https://www.strongtie.com/solutions/stainless-steel/fasteners): corrosion-resistant exterior fasteners. Product-specific screw spacing and pilot-hole guidance still needs to be checked for the actual selected screw.
- [University of Minnesota Extension: Raised bed gardens](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/raised-bed-gardens): permeable liner/drainage principle. Applying it to this elevated design is a design recommendation.
