# CedarSideHustle

## The idea

Build cedar planter boxes and similar items to sell through Facebook Marketplace and other suitable sales channels.

## First product

- **Initial size concept:** 14″ × 14″ × 24″ planter box. Prioritize five pickets and 14″ side pieces, allowing finished outside dimensions to be larger; actual dimensions still need verification against the construction method.
- **Material:** 5/8″ × 5-1/2″ × 6 ft incense cedar dog-ear fence pickets.
- **Tools and assembly:** miter saw, table saw, brad nailer, and drill/driver. Titebond III Ultimate recommended for exterior joints, with exterior screws recommended for bottom supports cut from leftover strips; final fastener specifications still to confirm.
- **Estimated lumber requirement:** five pickets per planter, using three dog-ear pieces for the bottom.
- **Lumber cost:** $17.40 before tax at the supplied price of $3.48 per picket; other materials and labor still need to be added.
- **Recommended test price:** $65 each; optional $120 pair offer after actual cost/time checks. This is a recommendation pending prototype validation.
- **Finish:** natural unfinished cedar for the first version.
- **Draft assembled size:** approximately 16″ × 16″ × 24-5/8″ using rotating butt joints and the original five-picket blank layout; verify on the prototype before listing dimensions.
- **Liner recommendation:** water-permeable landscape fabric, with drainage tested on the prototype.
- **Product details:** [[03 Business/CedarSideHustle/14x14x24 Cedar Planter|Cut list and product planning]].

## Seasonal product in development

- **Concept:** herringbone-style stacked cedar Christmas trees in two sizes, 6 ft and 4 ft, built from the same incense cedar pickets.
- **Layout:** one full-picket backbone (72″ or 48″) with a 7″ bare stub, plus two layers of 45°-mitered chevron branch pairs (5 tiers per layer on the 6 ft tree, 3 on the 4 ft).
- **Lumber:** 6 ft tree = 5 pickets ($17.40 before tax); 4 ft tree = 3 pickets ($10.44); one of each = 7 pickets ($24.36) by reusing the 6 ft leftovers.
- **Still open:** price, fastening, finish, base or stand, and prototype verification.

## Customer and proposed value

- **Initial customer hypothesis:** Kansas City metro homeowners and renters looking for planters for porches, patios, or small gardens; validate through actual inquiries.
- **Proposed value:** handmade cedar planters; appearance, durability, and price to be tested with a prototype and customer feedback.
- **Initial sales channel:** Facebook Marketplace.
- **Target area:** Kansas City, Missouri metro area.
- **Fulfillment:** pickup, an agreed meetup, or delivery; travel radius and fees still to set.
- **Other channels:** local groups where permitted and an OfferUp listing test; consider maker markets after proving demand. See [[03 Business/CedarSideHustle/Market Research|Market research]].

## Initial production approach

- **Confirmed preference:** begin by building a few planters, then decide between keeping stock and building to order based on demand.
- **Suggested starting quantity:** three builds total: one prototype for testing and two saleable planters after the prototype passes. This quantity is a recommendation, not a number specified by the user.
- **Lumber for three builds:** 15 pickets × $3.48 = **$52.20 before tax**. Buy/cut the first five for the prototype before committing the other ten to the same design.
- **Provisional supplies budget:** $75–$90 for three builds using the unverified $25–$30 per-planter material allowance; whole-package purchases may cost more up front and leave supplies for later builds. Labor, equipment, and travel are additional.
- Reserve the prototype for testing initially; do not count it as saleable inventory until its condition and suitability are assessed.
- Keep the first batch small until actual cost, production time, and customer response are known.

## Stock versus build to order

- Keep a few ready if buyers repeatedly want immediate pickup or orders arrive faster than replacement builds can be completed.
- Build to order if buyers accept the measured lead time and sales are intermittent.
- Use a hybrid if appropriate: keep one display/sample planter and a small number ready, then build additional orders on an agreed schedule.
- Record ready stock, committed orders, replenishment time, and time-to-sale; choose stock levels from those observations.

## Open planning items

- Other material costs and applicable purchase tax.
- Verify prototype geometry, bottom-support attachment, drainage, and brad compatibility.
- Measure hands-on build time and the total time spent on each sale.
- Set travel radius/fees per order and validate the recommended selling price.
