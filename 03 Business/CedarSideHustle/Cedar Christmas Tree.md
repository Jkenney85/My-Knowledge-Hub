# Cedar Christmas Tree

Seasonal product concept for [[03 Business/CedarSideHustle/CedarSideHustle - Overview|CedarSideHustle]], planned 2026-10-14. Related: [[03 Business/CedarSideHustle/14x14x24 Cedar Planter|Square planter]] · [[03 Business/CedarSideHustle/14x28x24 Cedar Planter|Rectangular planter]].

## Design status

Initial calculated layout only; not a physically verified build plan. The look comes from a TikTok video of a herringbone-style stacked cedar tree (Branch&Bow); the dimensions below are original calculations, not taken from the video. Two sizes are planned: **6 ft** and **4 ft**.

## Material and confirmed design rules

- **Stock:** [5/8″ × 5-1/2″ × 6 ft incense cedar dog-ear fence picket](https://www.homedepot.com/p/Sierra-Pacific-Industries-5-8-in-x-5-1-2-in-x-6-ft-Incense-Cedar-Dog-Ear-Wood-Fence-Picket-458830/202703421), the same stock as the planters. $3.48 each before tax, from the existing planter notes.
- **Backbone:** one full picket, dog-ear end up as the top of the tree. 72″ for the 6 ft tree; 48″ for the 4 ft tree.
- **Trunk stub:** 7″ of bare backbone at the bottom (confirmed).
- **Branches:** match the photo. Each tier is a chevron of two identical boards, one angled down-left and one down-right, meeting at the center of the backbone. Every board has 45° cuts on both ends.
- **Layers:** two. Layer 2 lies over layer 1, offset down by half a tier (5-1/2″). The 6 ft tree has 5 tiers per layer; the 4 ft tree has 3.
- **Top tier:** 11″ long side (confirmed). With 5-1/2″-wide boards and 45° ends, 11″ is the shortest long side that works (it comes to a point). The original 8″ idea would need the top-tier boards ripped to 4″ wide.
- **Size:** about 30″ wide at the bottom tier of the 6 ft tree (confirmed).

## Branch boards

Each board is a 5-1/2″-wide trapezoid with parallel 45° ends. Short side = long side − 11″. Left and right boards are identical, so cut one set and mirror by flipping the board. Width at tip is roughly 1.41 × the long side.

## Picket cutting layout

![[03 Business/CedarSideHustle/Assets/Christmas Tree Picket Cut Layouts.png]]

Neighboring pieces share one 45° cut, so each piece uses only its long side minus 5-1/2″ of stock. Alternate the miter direction on every cut; the only waste is a small triangle at the start of each picket.

### 6 ft tree: 5 pickets

| Picket | Cuts | Left over |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 72″ backbone, no cuts | none |
| 2–5 (all four identical) | One each of T5 21″, T4 18-1/2″, T3 16″, T2 13-1/2″, T1 11″ (58″ used) | about 14″ along the short edge, up to about 19-1/2″ along the long edge |

### 4 ft tree: 3 pickets

| Picket | Cuts |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 48″ backbone, then T3 16″ and T1 11″ from the 24″ offcut |
| 2 | Three T3 and two T2 (53″ used) |
| 3 | Two T2 and three T1 (38″ used) |

### Lumber and cost

| Build | Pickets | Cedar cost before tax |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6 ft tree | 5 | $17.40 |
| 4 ft tree | 3 | $10.44 |
| One of each, reusing 6 ft leftovers | 7 | $24.36 |

Building one of each separately takes 8 pickets ($27.84). Each leftover from pickets 2–5 of the 6 ft build holds one T2 (13-1/2″), so the four T2 boards for the 4 ft tree can come from those. The 4 ft tree then needs only two pickets: the backbone picket (T3 + T1) and one with three T3 and three T1 (about 53-1/2″ used).

### Kerf and trimming

- Allow about 1/8″ per saw cut; a 45° cut consumes about 3/16″ along the board.
- Trim up to about 1″ off the dog-ear end of each branch picket before the first cut.
- Picket 1 of the 4 ft tree has only about 2″ to spare after the backbone cut. If a cut goes wrong, move that T3 or T1 to a spare picket.
- Measure the actual stock and kerf before cutting; the layout assumes true 5-1/2″ width.

## Assumptions to verify

- **Arm angle:** the numbers assume arms hang 45° below horizontal, which is what a 45° center miter gives. The photo looks flatter; a flatter tree needs a center miter of about 55° and slightly different dimensions. Dry-fit one tier pair before cutting the full set.
- **Layer stacking:** both layers are assumed to sit on the same face of the backbone, giving an overall depth of about 1-7/8″ (backbone plus two 5/8″ layers).
- **Fastening and finish:** not decided. Glue and brads as on the planters is the likely approach; confirm how the chevron pairs attach to the backbone and how layer 2 attaches to layer 1.
- **Stand or base:** not decided. The 7″ stub could go into a base; stability of the upright tree is unverified.
