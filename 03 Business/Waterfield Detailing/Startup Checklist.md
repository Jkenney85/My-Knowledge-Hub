# Startup Checklist

Back to [[Waterfield Detailing - Overview|Waterfield Detailing]]

Not legal or insurance advice. Confirm details with the city, the state, and an insurance agent.

## 1. City rules (do this first)
- [ ] Call **Blue Springs Business Services: (816) 228-0131**, 903 W Main St.
- [ ] Ask specifically whether drop-offs are allowed, or only pickup and drop-off by me.

The city's Home-Based Business Guide says:
- A business license is required for most businesses, regardless of size.
- There can be no visible evidence of a home business and **no business signage** in residential neighborhoods.
- Home businesses **may not generate customer traffic**. Customers can't visit for services.
- No outdoor storage of business materials.
- No noise, glare, or other nuisances beyond normal residential levels, and no dumping or storing hazardous substances.

**What this means for the plan:** customer drop-offs look like a direct conflict. Pickup and drop-off by me may be fine, or may not. That's the question to ask. If driveway detailing isn't allowed at all, the fallback is going mobile to customers' driveways sooner (using their water and power).

- [ ] **City business license:** $50 per business + $2 per employee. [Apply here](https://www.bluespringsgov.com/406/Business-License)

## 2. HOA
- [ ] Read the covenants (CC&Rs) for rules on home businesses, commercial activity, signage, parking and vehicles stored in driveways.
- [ ] Check whether door hangers or flyers are allowed.

## 3. Legal structure
Options in Missouri:

| Option | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietor + fictitious name (DBA) | $7, valid 5 years | Simplest. No separation between business and personal liability. |
| LLC | $50 online ($105 by mail), one-time | No annual report or annual state fee in Missouri. Separates business liability from personal assets. Needs a registered agent (can be myself). |

- [ ] Decide on structure.
- [ ] If LLC: file Articles of Organization with the Missouri Secretary of State.
- [ ] If sole prop: register "Waterfield Detailing" as a fictitious name.
- [ ] Get a free EIN from the IRS (needed for most business bank accounts).
- [ ] Open a separate business bank account.
- [ ] Confirm with the Missouri Department of Revenue whether detailing services are subject to sales tax.

## 4. Insurance
Handling other people's cars is the biggest risk in this business. Ask an agent about:
- [ ] **General liability**: injuries or property damage.
- [ ] **Garagekeepers liability**: damage to customers' cars while in my care. This is the key one.
- [ ] **Driving customers' cars** for pickup and drop-off: will my personal auto policy cover this, or is a business auto / hired and non-owned auto policy needed? Get the answer in writing.

## 5. Customer protection
- [ ] Intake form: customer info, vehicle, service, pre-existing damage, valuables removed, sign-off.
- [ ] Walkaround photos before every job (all panels, wheels, interior, odometer, fuel level).
- [ ] After photos (these double as marketing).
